## 2. The EMAIL Formula for AI Drafting

Every professional email prompt should include these elements:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  THE EMAIL FORMULA                                                           │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                              │
│  E — EMOTION/TONE     How should this email feel? (professional, warm,      │
│                       apologetic, firm, excited, empathetic)                │
│                                                                              │
│  M — MESSAGE          What is the one core message? (request, update,       │
│                       apology, announcement, complaint, invitation)          │

│                                                                              │
```

## 3. Eight Types of Professional Emails — With Prompts

### 3.1 The Request Email

**Situation:** Asking someone for something — a meeting, approval, information, a referral.

**CRAFT Prompt:**
```
You are a professional [YOUR ROLE] at [COMPANY TYPE].

Draft an email requesting [WHAT YOU NEED] from [RECIPIENT — their role].
Context: [1–2 sentences explaining why you need this and any relevant background]
Your relationship: [first contact / existing relationship / internal colleague]

Structure:
- Subject line (clear and specific — not just "Request")
- Opening: Brief, direct context (1 sentence)
- The ask: State clearly what you want (1–2 sentences)
- Value to them / why they'd help: (1 sentence)
- Specific call to action with a deadline or timeframe
- Professional close

Tone: [Professional and direct / Warm and collegial / Formal and respectful]
Length: Under 120 words. Do not start with "I hope this email finds you well."
```

**Example Output:**
```
Subject: Meeting Request — AI Strategy Input for Q2 Planning

Hi Meera,

I'm developing our team's Q2 AI adoption roadmap and would value 30 minutes
of your insight — your recent work on the data governance framework is
directly relevant to the decisions we're working through.

Would you have time for a brief call between March 10–14? I'm flexible on
timing and happy to work around your schedule.

Thank you in advance — I'll make sure it's a good use of your time.

Best,
Rahul
```

---

### 3.2 The Follow-Up Email

**Situation:** Re-engaging someone who hasn't replied after 5–10 days.

**The challenge:** Must not feel nagging or passive-aggressive.

**CRAFT Prompt:**
```
You are a [ROLE] following up on [WHAT — proposal, meeting request, application].
Original email sent: [X days ago]
What you asked for: [BRIEF DESCRIPTION]
Their likely reason for not replying: [too busy / overlooked / still deciding]

Write a follow-up email that:
- References the original email briefly (1 sentence)
- Adds one piece of new value or context they didn't have before
- Makes it easy for them to reply (simple ask)
- Leaves the door open gracefully if timing is wrong

Tone: Light, professional, non-pushy. Zero passive aggression.
Do not say: "Just following up," "Circling back," "As per my last email."
Under 80 words.
```

**Example Output:**
```
Subject: Re: Partnership Discussion — Quick Note

Hi Priya,

Adding one thought to my earlier note: we just published a case study
showing how a company similar to yours reduced onboarding time by 40%
with our platform — might be relevant to the initiative you mentioned.

Happy to share it alongside the overview I sent. If Q1 timing doesn't work,
I'm equally happy to connect in April.

Rohan
```

### 3.3 The Apology / Escalation Resolution Email

**Situation:** Something went wrong and you need to apologize — to a client, manager, or colleague.

**The challenge:** Must be genuine (not corporate), take clear ownership, offer a solution.

**CRAFT Prompt:**
```
You are a [ROLE] writing an apology email for: [DESCRIBE WHAT WENT WRONG]
Recipient: [Their role and relationship to you]
Impact on them: [What inconvenience/problem did this cause?]
What you are doing to fix it: [SPECIFIC ACTION]
What you are doing to prevent recurrence: [IF APPLICABLE]

Write an apology email that:
- Leads with the apology (first sentence — not buried)
- Takes clear ownership (no "mistakes were made" passive voice)
- Acknowledges the specific impact on them
- States the concrete action being taken
- Expresses commitment without over-promising

Tone: Sincere and professional. NOT defensive. NOT over-apologetic.
Avoid: "We apologize for any inconvenience caused" — be specific.
Under 150 words.
```

**Example Output:**
```
Subject: Apology — Delay in Your Project Deliverable

Dear Ananya,

I owe you a direct apology. The first-draft analysis I committed to deliver
by Thursday arrived Saturday morning — that delay disrupted your client
review, and I take full responsibility.

I've completed the work and attached it now. To prevent this in future,
I've built buffer time into my project scheduling and will send a proactive
heads-up if a deadline is at risk — rather than missing it silently.

Thank you for your patience. I value this engagement and your trust, and
I'm committed to making the rest of the project seamless.

Sincerely,
Karan
```

### 3.4 The Difficult Conversation Email

**Situation:** Raising a concern, delivering bad news, or addressing a conflict in writing.

**CRAFT Prompt:**
```
You are a [ROLE] who needs to communicate something difficult to [RECIPIENT].
The difficult message: [DESCRIBE THE SITUATION]
Your goal: [What outcome do you want — understanding, change in behavior, decision reversal?]
Your relationship: [Peer / Manager / Report / Client]

Write an email that:
- Opens by acknowledging the relationship positively
- States the concern directly but without blame language
- Uses "I observed" or "I noticed" rather than "you did X wrong"
- Proposes a constructive path forward
- Invites dialogue rather than closing the conversation

Tone: Respectful, direct, emotionally intelligent. Not passive-aggressive.
Do not: Soften the message so much the point is lost.
Under 200 words.
```

---

### 3.5 The Introduction / Networking Email

**Situation:** Introducing yourself to a new contact — cold outreach or warm introduction.

**CRAFT Prompt:**
```
Write a professional introduction / outreach email.
I am: [YOUR ROLE AND BACKGROUND — 1–2 sentences]
I am reaching out to: [THEIR NAME, ROLE, COMPANY]
Connection point (if any): [Mutual contact / Read their article / Saw their talk]
What I want: [A conversation / advice / to explore collaboration]
What I offer them: [Why this conversation is worth their time]

Requirements:
- Subject line: Personal and specific — not "Introduction" or "Networking"
- Open with a genuine, specific observation about their work
- Be clear about why you're reaching out in 1–2 sentences
- Make a specific, low-friction ask (15-min call / brief email reply)
- Keep it about them more than about you

Under 120 words. Do not use flattery. Be genuine.
```

---

### 3.6 The Complaint Email

**Situation:** Raising a complaint to a vendor, service provider, or internal team.

**CRAFT Prompt:**
```
Write a professional complaint email.
Situation: [DESCRIBE WHAT WENT WRONG AND WHEN]
Impact: [HOW HAS THIS AFFECTED YOU / YOUR BUSINESS]
What I want resolved: [SPECIFIC RESOLUTION — refund, fix, escalation, explanation]
My relationship with them: [Paying customer / Long-term partner / New vendor]

Structure:
1. State the problem specifically (dates, amounts, what was promised vs. delivered)
2. State the impact clearly and factually
3. State what resolution you expect and by when
4. Note what you will do if unresolved (escalate, seek alternative)

Tone: Firm, professional, factual. Not emotional or threatening.
No vague language — every claim with a specific detail.
Under 200 words.
```

---

### 3.7 The Proposal / Pitch Email

**Situation:** Proposing an idea, project, collaboration, or solution to a decision-maker.

**CRAFT Prompt:**
```
You are a [ROLE] pitching [IDEA/PROJECT/PROPOSAL] to [DECISION-MAKER ROLE].
Their primary concern: [What matters most to them — cost / risk / speed / quality]
My proposal in one sentence: [WHAT YOU ARE PROPOSING]
Key benefit to them: [QUANTIFIED IF POSSIBLE]
What I need from them: [Approval / Meeting / Feedback / Budget]

Write a pitch email:
- Subject: Make them want to open it (benefit-led, not topic-led)
- Opening: Their pain point or opportunity (1 sentence — about them, not you)
- The proposal: What you're suggesting and why now (2–3 sentences)
- Evidence: One specific proof point or example (1–2 sentences)
- Clear, confident call to action

Tone: Confident and value-focused. Not salesy or desperate.
Under 150 words.
```

---

### 3.8 The Status / Progress Update Email

**Situation:** Keeping stakeholders informed on an ongoing project or initiative.

**CRAFT Prompt:**
```
Write a project/work status update email.
Project: [NAME]
Audience: [Manager / Client / Team / Stakeholders]
Period covered: [This week / This month / Q3]
Status: [On track / At risk / Delayed / Complete]

Key updates:
1. [MILESTONE — status]
2. [MILESTONE — status]
3. [ISSUE or BLOCKER — if any]
Next steps: [What happens next and by when]

Format:
- Subject: [Project] Status — [Period] — [Status emoji: ✅ 🟡 🔴]
- 3-paragraph structure: (1) overall status, (2) key updates, (3) next steps
- One-line action required from reader (if any)

Tone: Transparent, professional, concise.
Under 200 words. Do not start with "I wanted to update you on..."
```

## 4. AI for Email Editing — Improving What You Already Wrote

### 4.1 The Four Most Valuable Email Editing Prompts

**Prompt 1 — Compress Without Losing Meaning:**
```
Shorten this email by 40% without losing any important information.
Keep the core message, the ask, and the key evidence.
Remove: redundant phrases, filler sentences, excessive politeness.
[PASTE EMAIL]
```

**Prompt 2 — Tone Adjustment:**
```
This email is currently too [formal / casual / aggressive / apologetic].
Rewrite it to be more [professional / warm / direct / confident] 
while keeping all the content and the core message exactly the same.
[PASTE EMAIL]
```

**Prompt 3 — Clarity Improvement:**
```
Review this email for clarity. Identify:
1. Any sentence that could be misunderstood
2. Any point where the ask is unclear
3. Any jargon the recipient may not understand
Then rewrite those specific sections only. Leave the rest unchanged.
[PASTE EMAIL]
```

**Prompt 4 — Subject Line Generator:**
```
Generate 5 alternative subject lines for this email.
Requirements:
- Under 50 characters each
- Specific and descriptive (not vague)
- The best 2 should create a sense of value or urgency
- Do not use clickbait language
[PASTE EMAIL BODY]
```

---

### 4.2 The Full Email Review Prompt

Use this to get a comprehensive review of any important email before sending:

```
Review this email before I send it. Evaluate:
1. CLARITY: Is the core message and ask crystal clear?
2. TONE: Is the tone appropriate for this recipient and situation?
3. LENGTH: Is it the right length or does it need trimming?
4. OPENING: Does it avoid weak openers ("I hope...", "Just checking...")?
5. CLOSING: Is the call to action specific and actionable?
6. RED FLAGS: Any phrases that could be misinterpreted or feel off?

Then provide:
- A revised version incorporating your suggestions
- One sentence explaining the most important change you made

[PASTE EMAIL]
```

## 5. Maintaining Your Authentic Voice

### 5.1 The Voice Calibration Technique

AI drafts sometimes sound generic. To get output that sounds like *you*, provide your voice as a reference.

```
PROMPT:
Study these 3 emails I wrote. Identify my writing style:
[PASTE EMAIL 1]
[PASTE EMAIL 2]
[PASTE EMAIL 3]

Then describe my style in 5 bullet points (vocabulary, sentence length,
tone, typical structure, any signature phrases I use).

Now write a new email in my exact style for:
[NEW EMAIL TASK]
```

### 5.2 The "Sound Like Me" Constraint

Add to any email prompt:

```
After drafting, apply my writing style:
- I write in short, direct sentences
- I use informal but professional language
- I almost never use exclamation marks
- I sign off with "Best, [Name]" — not "Regards" or "Sincerely"
- I never use "please find attached" — I say "I've attached..."
```

## 7. Industry Case Study: AI Email at HubSpot

**Company:** HubSpot — CRM and marketing platform, 6,000+ employees

**Challenge:** Sales Development Representatives (SDRs) were spending 60% of their time writing personalized outreach emails — leaving only 40% for actual sales activities (calls, demos, relationship building).

**The AI Solution:** HubSpot integrated AI email drafting (powered by ChatGPT/GPT-4) directly into their CRM:
- AI reads the prospect's company, industry, role, and recent news
- Generates a personalized outreach email using this context
- SDR reviews, personalizes further, and sends in 2 minutes vs. 15 minutes

**Results:**
- Email drafting time: 15 min → 2 min per email (87% reduction)
- SDRs went from 20 personalized emails/day to 60+ personalized emails/day
- Reply rate maintained at the same level (AI personalization was as effective as human)
- More time for calls and demos → 34% increase in meetings booked
- No SDR headcount reduction — productivity tripled

**The key finding:** Personalization quality mattered more than who wrote the email. AI-assisted emails that were genuinely personalized (using real context about the prospect) performed identically to human-written emails — at 7x the volume.

## 9. Responsible AI Use in Email Communication

### 9.1 Email Ethics Checklist

```
Before sending any AI-assisted email:

☐ Have I read the full email as if I were the recipient?
☐ Does this email accurately represent my actual position and intentions?
☐ Would I be comfortable if the recipient knew AI helped draft this?
☐ Have I removed any factual claims AI might have added that I can't verify?
☐ Does the email match the tone of my actual relationship with this person?
☐ For sensitive topics (apology, complaint, difficult news): Does this
  sound genuinely human, or too polished/corporate?
☐ Am I still taking full responsibility for this communication?
```

### 9.2 When to Write Emails Yourself (Without AI Drafting)

Some situations warrant fully human-written communication:
- Deeply personal condolences or support
- Very high-stakes relationship moments (first contact with a key executive)
- Situations requiring authentic emotional vulnerability
- Anything where the recipient would feel cheated if they knew AI wrote it

AI drafts are best for: **repetitive, time-consuming, structured professional communication** — not for replacing genuine human connection.
