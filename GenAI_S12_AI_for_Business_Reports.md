## 1. The Business Document Problem

### 1.1 Where Professionals Lose Time in Document Work

```
RESEARCH: Professional document creation time (McKinsey, 2023)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Report writing:          3.5 hours average per report
  Proposal drafting:       6–12 hours per proposal
  Policy/SOP creation:     4–8 hours per document
  Executive summary:       45–90 minutes (even with content)
  Business case:           8–16 hours

  Total: A typical manager produces 2–3 of these per month
  Monthly cost: 30–60 hours on document creation alone

  AI can reduce first-draft time by 70–85%
  Human time then focuses on: accuracy checking, judgment,
  stakeholder-specific nuance, and final polish
```

### 1.2 The AI-Assisted Document Workflow

```
WITHOUT AI:
  Blank page → Outline (1 hr) → Research (2 hrs) → Draft (3 hrs) 
  → Edit (1 hr) → Polish (30 min) = 7.5 hours

WITH AI:
  Notes/data → AI generates outline (5 min) → AI drafts sections (15 min)
  → Human reviews, corrects, adds judgment (45 min) 
  → AI polishes (10 min) = 75 minutes

  Time saving: 85%
  Quality improvement: Structure is more rigorous; 
  first draft is better than most human first drafts
```

## 2. The Business Report

### 2.1 Report Structure — The Gold Standard

Every business report, regardless of length, should follow this structure:

```
BUSINESS REPORT STRUCTURE
──────────────────────────
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY        ← Decision-makers read ONLY this
   • Situation (1 sentence)
   • Key finding (1–2 sentences)
   • Recommendation (1 sentence)
   • Impact if acted upon (1 sentence)

2. BACKGROUND / CONTEXT     ← Why this report was written
   • The business problem or question
   • Scope and time period
   • Data sources used

3. METHODOLOGY             ← How the analysis was conducted
   • What data was collected
   • How it was analyzed
   • Any limitations

4. FINDINGS               ← What the data shows
   • Presented logically (most to least important)
   • Evidence for each finding
   • Visualizations or tables (if applicable)

5. ANALYSIS               ← What the findings MEAN
   • Interpretation and implications
   • Root causes
   • Patterns and connections

6. RECOMMENDATIONS        ← What to DO
   • Specific, actionable, prioritized
   • Resources required
   • Timeline

7. APPENDICES             ← Supporting detail
   • Raw data, detailed calculations, methodology notes
```

### 2.2 AI Report Generation — Phased Approach

**Phase 1: Generate the Structure**

```
PROMPT:
You are a Senior Business Analyst with expertise in [INDUSTRY/DOMAIN].

Create a detailed report structure for a report titled:
"[REPORT TITLE]"

Context: [WHO COMMISSIONED IT, WHAT QUESTION IT ANSWERS, FOR WHAT AUDIENCE]
Scope: [TIME PERIOD, GEOGRAPHIC SCOPE, BUSINESS UNITS COVERED]

Generate:
1. A complete table of contents with section and sub-section headings
2. For each section: a 1-sentence description of what it covers
3. Suggested length allocation (approximate word count per section)
4. Indicate which sections require primary data vs. can use AI analysis

Total report length target: [1,000 / 2,000 / 5,000 words]
```

---

**Phase 2: Draft Individual Sections**

```
PROMPT (for each section):
You are drafting Section [X]: [SECTION TITLE] of a business report for
[AUDIENCE — e.g., senior management of a retail company].

Context established in earlier sections:
[Brief summary of what has been established — paste prior section summaries]

Here is the data/information for this section:
[PASTE YOUR RAW DATA, NOTES, OR BULLET POINTS]

Write this section in formal business report style:
- Paragraph prose (not bullet points) unless findings section
- Evidence-based — cite the data I provided
- Do not add information not in my notes — flag gaps as [DATA NEEDED]
- Professional, authoritative tone
- Target: [WORD COUNT]
```

---

**Phase 3: Generate the Executive Summary Last**

```
PROMPT:
You have now completed all sections of the report. Here are the key points
from each section:
[PASTE KEY POINTS FROM ALL SECTIONS]

Write a standalone executive summary that:
- Can be understood without reading the full report
- Answers: What was the situation? What did we find? What should we do?
- Includes the single most important number or fact
- Ends with a clear recommendation
- Is written for a C-suite executive who has 90 seconds to read it
- Is under 150 words

Do not use jargon. Use active voice throughout.
```

### 2.3 Example: A Real Report Prompt in Action

**Scenario:** You need to write a "Customer Satisfaction Analysis Q3" report for your company's monthly management meeting.

**Your raw notes:**
```
- NPS dropped from 54 to 47 in Q3
- Biggest driver: delivery time (avg 5.2 days vs 3.8 days promised)
- Customer service rating actually improved (3.8 → 4.1/5)
- Top complaint: packaging damage (23% of negative feedback)
- Highest satisfaction: product quality (mentioned positively in 68% of reviews)
- Survey: 380 respondents, 82% response rate
- Competitor X's NPS is 62 (from public data)
```

**AI prompt:**
```
You are a Senior Customer Experience Analyst writing a formal report for
the Operations and Marketing leadership team (non-technical audience).

I have collected Q3 customer satisfaction data. Here are my raw findings:
[PASTE NOTES ABOVE]

Write Section 3: Key Findings of this report.

Structure this section as:
3.1 Overall Satisfaction Score
3.2 Top Drivers of Dissatisfaction (ranked by frequency)
3.3 Top Drivers of Satisfaction
3.4 Benchmarking vs. Industry

For each sub-section:
- 1 topic sentence stating the key point
- 2–3 sentences of explanation with data
- Implications for the business (1 sentence)

Business report tone — professional prose, not bullets.
400 words maximum for this section.
Do not add information not in my notes.
```

## 3. The Executive Summary

The executive summary is the most read and most neglected document in business. AI excels at generating tight, decision-ready summaries.

### 3.1 The Executive Summary Formula

```
SITUATION:    [1 sentence] What is the context?
PROBLEM/OPP: [1 sentence] What specifically needs attention?
FINDING:      [1–2 sentences] What does the data show?
RECOMMENDATION: [1 sentence] What should be done?
IMPACT:       [1 sentence] What happens if we act on this?

Total: 5 sentences / ~100 words
```

### 3.2 The "Distill Any Document" Prompt

```
PROMPT:
Read the following document and write an executive summary.

Rules for the summary:
- 5 sentences maximum
- Sentence 1: The situation (context — 1 sentence)
- Sentence 2: The core problem or opportunity
- Sentence 3: The key finding or evidence
- Sentence 4: The recommendation
- Sentence 5: The expected outcome or consequence of inaction
- Written for a senior executive who will make a decision based on this alone
- Use active voice. No jargon. No hedging.

DOCUMENT:
[PASTE YOUR DOCUMENT HERE]
```

### 3.3 The "Reverse Brief" Technique

If you have only bullet points (not a finished document):

```
PROMPT:
Convert these bullet-point findings into a polished 150-word executive summary
that a CFO would use to decide whether to approve this project.

Format:
Paragraph 1: Situation and key finding (2–3 sentences)
Paragraph 2: Recommendation with supporting rationale (2–3 sentences)
Paragraph 3: Resource requirement and expected ROI (1–2 sentences)

DATA POINTS:
[PASTE YOUR BULLET POINTS]

Tone: Confident and direct. No qualifiers. Numbers where possible.
```

## 4. The Business Proposal

### 4.1 Proposal Structure — The Client-First Architecture

The fatal mistake in business proposals: leading with your company and capabilities instead of the client's problem and your solution.

```
WINNING PROPOSAL STRUCTURE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. COVER PAGE          — Professional, branded, client name prominent
2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY   — Their problem + your solution + ROI (1 page)
3. SITUATION ANALYSIS  — Show you understand their problem deeply
4. PROPOSED SOLUTION   — What you will do, how, with what methodology
5. DELIVERABLES        — Concrete outputs they will receive
6. TIMELINE            — Phased plan with milestones
7. INVESTMENT          — Pricing, ROI calculation, payment terms
8. ABOUT US            — Brief credentials and relevant experience
9. CASE STUDIES        — Proof that you've done this before
10. NEXT STEPS         — Clear call to action; how to proceed
```

### 4.2 AI Proposal Drafting Prompts

**Prompt 1 — Situation Analysis:**
```
You are a senior consultant writing a business proposal for a client.

Write the "Situation Analysis" section showing you deeply understand
the client's challenges. The client is:
[DESCRIBE CLIENT — industry, size, current challenges]

Based on this profile, write:
1. The current state they are in (3 specific pain points we assume are true)
2. The business consequences of these pain points (what it costs them)
3. The opportunity: what becomes possible if these are solved
4. A bridge sentence into the solution section

Tone: Empathetic and authoritative. Show understanding, not assumption.
Flag any statement I should verify with the actual client as [VERIFY].
350 words.
```

**Prompt 2 — Solution Section:**
```
Write the "Proposed Solution" section for a business proposal.

We are proposing: [DESCRIBE YOUR SOLUTION / SERVICE]
Client's primary pain points: [FROM SITUATION ANALYSIS]
Our methodology: [DESCRIBE YOUR APPROACH / FRAMEWORK]
Key differentiator from alternatives: [WHAT MAKES YOUR APPROACH UNIQUE]

Structure:
- Solution overview (2–3 sentences)
- Our methodology / approach (3–4 sentences + optional phases)
- Why our approach specifically addresses their situation (2–3 sentences)
- What the client will experience during the engagement (brief)

Tone: Confident and specific. Avoid vague consultant-speak.
Do not say "bespoke," "robust," "holistic," or "leverage."
400 words.
```

**Prompt 3 — ROI / Investment Section:**
```
Write the "Return on Investment" section for a business proposal.

Our investment ask: [PRICE]
Client's current cost of the problem (estimated): [COST]
Expected outcomes after working with us:
  - [OUTCOME 1 — quantified if possible]
  - [OUTCOME 2 — quantified if possible]
  - [OUTCOME 3 — quantified if possible]
Timeline to see results: [TIMEFRAME]

Write a compelling ROI narrative that:
1. States the investment
2. Frames it against the cost of inaction
3. Projects the expected return over [12 / 24 / 36 months]
4. Presents a conservative, base, and optimistic scenario
5. Ends with a clear payback period statement

Tone: Financial precision. Evidence-based. No hype.
250 words.
```

## 5. Other Key Document Types

### 5.1 Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

```
PROMPT:
You are a Process Documentation Specialist.

Write a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for: [PROCESS NAME]
Department: [DEPARTMENT]
Purpose: [WHY THIS SOP EXISTS]
Frequency: [HOW OFTEN THIS PROCESS IS PERFORMED]
Key steps I know are involved: [BULLET LIST YOUR ROUGH STEPS]

SOP Format:
1. PURPOSE (1 sentence)
2. SCOPE (who this applies to)
3. RESPONSIBILITIES (roles involved)
4. DEFINITIONS (any terms that need clarifying)
5. PROCEDURE (numbered step-by-step — clear action + responsible party + output)
6. EXCEPTIONS (what to do if X, Y, Z happens differently)
7. REVISION HISTORY (date, version, change summary)

Tone: Clear, imperative voice ("Obtain approval from..." not "The manager should...")
No ambiguous language. Every step must be actionable.
```

### 5.2 Business Case

```
PROMPT:
Write a 1-page business case for the following proposal:
Initiative: [WHAT YOU WANT TO DO]
Problem being solved: [CURRENT SITUATION]
Proposed investment: [COST AND RESOURCES REQUIRED]
Expected benefit: [WHAT WILL IMPROVE AND BY HOW MUCH]
Timeline: [FROM APPROVAL TO RESULTS]
Risks: [WHAT COULD GO WRONG]
Alternatives considered: [OTHER OPTIONS EVALUATED]

Format:
- Executive Summary (3 sentences)
- Problem Statement (1 paragraph)
- Proposed Solution (1 paragraph)
- Cost-Benefit Analysis (table: costs vs. benefits over 3 years)
- Risks and Mitigation (3 bullets)
- Recommendation (1 sentence)

Audience: Senior management committee approving budget.
Tone: Objective, evidence-based, business-focused.
Maximum 500 words.
```

### 5.3 Policy Document

```
PROMPT:
You are an HR/Legal Policy Specialist.

Draft a [POLICY TYPE] policy document for [COMPANY TYPE].
This policy covers: [SCOPE OF THE POLICY]
Key rules and requirements: [BULLET LIST YOUR REQUIREMENTS]
Exceptions and special cases: [IF ANY]

Policy Document Format:
1. POLICY TITLE AND NUMBER
2. EFFECTIVE DATE
3. PURPOSE (why this policy exists)
4. SCOPE (who it applies to)
5. POLICY STATEMENT (the rules, numbered)
6. RESPONSIBILITIES (by role)
7. CONSEQUENCES OF NON-COMPLIANCE
8. EXCEPTIONS PROCESS
9. REVIEW CYCLE
10. APPROVAL AND SIGN-OFF

Tone: Clear, formal, unambiguous. Policies must be interpretable without
explanation. Every rule must be specific enough to enforce.
Flag any section where legal review is recommended as [LEGAL REVIEW NEEDED].
```

## 6. Document Quality Control with AI

### 6.1 The Pre-Submission Review Prompt

Before submitting any important document, run this review:

```
PROMPT:
You are a senior editor reviewing this business document before submission.

Review for:
1. CLARITY: Any sentence that is ambiguous or could be misread
2. LOGIC: Any gap in reasoning or unsupported claim
3. ACCURACY: Any statement that appears to be guessed rather than evidenced
   (flag with [VERIFY])
4. TONE: Any section that is too informal, too aggressive, or too passive
5. STRUCTURE: Does the document flow logically? Any section out of place?
6. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: Does it accurately represent what follows?

Provide:
- A list of specific issues found with the line or section reference
- Suggested corrections for each
- An overall quality rating: READY TO SUBMIT / NEEDS MINOR REVISION / NEEDS MAJOR REVISION

DOCUMENT:
[PASTE DOCUMENT]
```

### 6.2 The Simplification Prompt

For documents going to non-expert audiences:

```
PROMPT:
Simplify this document for a non-specialist audience.

Rules:
- Replace every technical term with plain English
- Break up sentences longer than 25 words
- Convert passive voice to active
- Replace abstract nouns with concrete verbs
  (e.g., "the implementation of changes" → "we changed")
- Add a 2-sentence plain-English summary at the top of each section

Do NOT remove any important information — simplify the language, not the content.

[PASTE DOCUMENT]
```

## 7. Real-World Example: Deloitte's AI-Assisted Consulting Reports

**Company:** Deloitte — Big 4 consulting and professional services

**The Challenge:**
Consulting teams spent enormous time writing client reports that followed predictable structures but required significant customization. Junior consultants spent 60–70% of their time on document production rather than analysis.

**The Solution — AI-Assisted Report Generation:**

```
DELOITTE'S AI DOCUMENT WORKFLOW:
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Phase 1: Engagement planning
  → AI generates report skeleton from engagement scope document
  → Senior consultant reviews and approves structure (20 min)

Phase 2: Data collection and analysis
  → Consultants collect and analyze data (human + AI analysis)
  → AI converts analyst notes into structured findings paragraphs

Phase 3: Report drafting
  → AI generates full first draft from findings and structure
  → Junior consultant reviews for accuracy (2 hours)
  → Manager reviews and refines for strategic narrative (1 hour)

Phase 4: Client review
  → Partner reviews final document (30 min)
  → Client receives polished, professional report
```

**Results:**
- Report production time: 40 hours → 8 hours (80% reduction)
- Junior consultants: freed from document production → more analytical work
- Document consistency: higher — AI applies the same standard structure every time
- Client satisfaction: improved — faster delivery, more time spent on insights
- Revenue per project maintained while engagement cost reduced → higher margin

## 9. Interview Questions — Session 12

**Q1:** *"How would you use AI to help write a business report?"*

**Strong Answer:**
"I use a phased approach. First, I feed AI my raw notes, data, and brief to generate a structured report outline with section descriptions — this ensures the structure is rigorous before any writing begins. Then I draft section by section, using AI to convert my raw findings and analysis notes into polished business prose — always instructing it to flag any gaps as [DATA NEEDED] rather than fill in information I haven't provided. I write the executive summary last, after all sections are drafted, using a concise 5-sentence formula. Finally, I run a pre-submission review prompt to catch logical gaps, unsupported claims, and tone issues. This reduces first-draft time by about 80% while keeping me in full control of accuracy and judgment."
