## 2. Proven Presentation Narrative Frameworks

### 2.1 The Problem-Solution-Impact (PSI) Structure

The most common and effective structure for business presentations:

```
SLIDE FLOW:
1. HOOK            — Attention-grabbing opening (stat, story, question)
2. PROBLEM         — The situation/challenge the audience faces
3. CONSEQUENCES    — What happens if the problem isn't solved
4. SOLUTION        — Your answer/proposal/recommendation
5. HOW IT WORKS    — Methodology, approach, plan
6. EVIDENCE        — Proof it works (case study, data, testimonial)
7. CALL TO ACTION  — What you need the audience to decide or do
```

**AI Prompt — PSI Structure Generation:**
```
You are a presentation strategist who creates compelling business decks.

Create a 10-slide presentation outline using the PSI narrative structure for:
Topic: [YOUR TOPIC]
Audience: [WHO WILL WATCH — role, knowledge level, decision-making power]
My goal: [WHAT DO I WANT THEM TO DO AFTER THIS PRESENTATION?]
Key message: [THE ONE THING I WANT THEM TO REMEMBER]
Main evidence I have: [YOUR KEY DATA POINTS OR EXAMPLES]

For each slide, provide:
- Slide number and title (action-oriented — a claim, not a label)
- 3 bullet points (max 10 words each) OR the slide's visual concept
- Speaker note: 2–3 sentences elaborating on this slide
```

---

### 2.2 The Situation-Complication-Resolution (SCR) Structure

Used by McKinsey and top consulting firms for executive briefings:

```
SITUATION:      What is the current state? (shared understanding)
COMPLICATION:   What has changed, gone wrong, or created urgency?
RESOLUTION:     What is the recommended course of action?

SLIDE FLOW:
1. SITUATION    — "We are currently..."
2. COMPLICATION — "However, [something has changed/gone wrong]..."
3. RESOLUTION   — "Therefore, we recommend..."
4. PROOF        — Evidence supporting the recommendation
5. NEXT STEPS   — Specific actions with owners and dates
```

**AI Prompt — SCR Structure:**
```
Write a 7-slide executive presentation outline using the
Situation-Complication-Resolution (SCR) framework.

Situation: [DESCRIBE THE CURRENT STATE — facts the audience accepts]
Complication: [WHAT HAS CHANGED OR WHAT PROBLEM HAS EMERGED]
Resolution: [YOUR RECOMMENDATION OR PROPOSED SOLUTION]
Evidence: [YOUR KEY DATA OR EXAMPLES]
Audience: Senior executives (C-suite). They have limited time and want conclusions first.

For each slide:
- Slide title: A complete sentence that makes your point (not a label)
- Content: Maximum 3 bullets OR one key visual concept description
- Speaker talking point: 1–2 sentences only
```

---

### 2.3 The Data Storytelling Structure

For presentations centered on data and analysis:

```
1. THE QUESTION     — Start with the business question the data answers
2. THE DATA         — Present the key finding (one chart, one message)
3. THE STORY        — What story does this data tell?
4. THE IMPLICATION  — What does this mean for our decisions?
5. THE ACTION       — What specific action does this call for?
```

**The Barbara Minto Pyramid Principle for slides:**

```
Each slide follows this rule:
  TITLE:   The conclusion (what you want them to believe after this slide)
  BODY:    Evidence that supports the conclusion
  VISUAL:  One chart/image that makes the evidence unmistakable

WRONG:     Title = "Q3 Revenue Performance"
RIGHT:     Title = "Q3 Revenue Grew 18% — Fastest Growth in 3 Years"

WRONG:     Title = "Customer Feedback Summary"
RIGHT:     Title = "Delivery Speed Is Our #1 Customer Complaint — Up 40% This Quarter"
```

## 3. AI Slide Content Generation

### 3.1 The Slide-by-Slide Prompt

Generate content for each slide individually for highest quality:

```
PROMPT:
You are a professional presentation writer creating a slide for an executive audience.

Slide: [SLIDE NUMBER] — [SLIDE TITLE]
Context: This slide comes after: [BRIEF SUMMARY OF PREVIOUS SLIDE]
This slide needs to: [PURPOSE — prove a point / introduce a concept / show data]
My raw content: [PASTE YOUR NOTES OR DATA FOR THIS SLIDE]

Generate:
1. HEADLINE (the slide title): One complete sentence that states your conclusion.
   Under 10 words. An assertion, not a label.
2. BODY CONTENT: 3 bullet points. Each under 12 words. Each supports the headline.
3. VISUAL SUGGESTION: Describe a chart, image, or diagram that would make
   this slide more powerful. Be specific (e.g., "bar chart comparing X vs Y" —
   not "a relevant graph").
4. SPEAKER NOTES: 3–5 sentences you would say aloud. Adds context and 
   nuance that isn't on the slide.
```

---

### 3.2 Transforming Documents into Presentations

Convert an existing document (report, proposal, analysis) into a presentation:

```
PROMPT:
You are a presentation designer. Convert this business document into a
structured 12-slide presentation outline.

AUDIENCE: [WHO WILL SEE THIS — different from the report audience?]
CONTEXT: [Why are you presenting this? What decision needs to be made?]
KEY CONSTRAINT: [What is the ONE most important message?]

Rules for conversion:
- Each slide must have an assertion title (not a label)
- Maximum 3 bullets per slide, 12 words max each
- The narrative must flow logically — each slide leads to the next
- Cut everything that isn't essential for the audience's decision
- Suggest one visual/chart for each data-heavy slide

DOCUMENT:
[PASTE YOUR DOCUMENT HERE]
```

### 3.3 Slide Title Transformation — From Label to Assertion

The single most impactful improvement AI can make to most presentations:

**Prompt:**
```
These are current slide titles from my presentation. They are labels, not assertions.
Transform each into a complete sentence that states the key conclusion of that slide.
The title should make the audience think "I need to understand more about this."

Current titles:
1. Market Overview
2. Competitor Analysis
3. Customer Survey Results
4. Financial Performance
5. Recommendations

Transform to: [Action title — a clear assertion that communicates the finding]
```

**Example transformation:**

| Label Title (Weak) | Assertion Title (Strong) |
|-------------------|-------------------------|
| Market Overview | "Indian EV Market Will Triple by 2028 — Entry Window Is Now" |
| Competitor Analysis | "Our Top Competitor's Weakness Is Speed — We Can Win on 48-Hour Delivery" |
| Customer Survey Results | "Customers Love Our Product Quality But Leave Over Delivery Experience" |
| Financial Performance | "Q3 Margin Improved 200 bps — On Track to Exceed Annual Target" |
| Recommendations | "Three Actions in 90 Days Will Capture the Growth Opportunity" |

## 4. Speaker Notes and Q&A Preparation

### 4.1 Speaker Notes Generator

```
PROMPT:
Generate speaker notes for this presentation slide.

Slide title: "[ASSERTION TITLE]"
Slide content (bullets): [LIST YOUR 3 BULLETS]
Context: This slide is presented to [AUDIENCE] during [OCCASION].
This slide comes after [PREVIOUS SLIDE TOPIC].

Speaker notes should:
- Expand on the bullets (don't repeat them word for word)
- Add a specific example or data point not on the slide
- Explain WHY this matters for this specific audience
- Include a transition line to the next slide
- Be natural to speak aloud (conversational, not formal prose)

Duration: Enough notes for 90 seconds of speaking.
```

### 4.2 Q&A Preparation — Anti-Fragile Presenting

The best presentations are those where the presenter has anticipated every hard question. AI makes this easy:

```
PROMPT:
You are a skeptical board member / senior executive reviewing this presentation.
Generate the 10 toughest questions you would ask after watching it.

For each question:
- State the question as a senior stakeholder would ask it (not gently)
- Indicate which slide/section it challenges
- Rate it: EASY / MEDIUM / HARD to answer

My presentation covers: [BRIEF SUMMARY — 3–5 sentences]
Key claims I make: [LIST YOUR 3–5 MOST IMPORTANT CLAIMS]
My audience: [WHO IS WATCHING]

PRIORITIZE: Questions that challenge my data, my assumptions, my recommendations,
and the feasibility of implementation.
```

**Then for each hard question:**
```
PROMPT:
Prepare a 60-second spoken answer to this question:
"[PASTE THE HARD QUESTION]"

Context of my presentation: [BRIEF CONTEXT]
What I know: [RELEVANT DATA OR FACTS YOU HAVE]
What I DON'T know (be honest): [GAPS IN YOUR KNOWLEDGE]

Format:
- 1 sentence: Acknowledge the validity of the question
- 2–3 sentences: Your direct answer with evidence
- 1 sentence: What you would do to strengthen this answer if given more time
- 1 sentence: Bridge back to your recommendation

Under 120 words. Confident, not defensive.
```

## 6. Adapting Presentations for Different Audiences

### 6.1 The Audience Calibration Prompt

```
PROMPT:
I have a presentation about [TOPIC]. Here is my current slide outline:
[PASTE YOUR CURRENT OUTLINE]

I need to adapt this for a DIFFERENT audience. Original audience: [ORIGINAL].
New audience: [NEW AUDIENCE — role, knowledge level, concerns]

Please adapt the presentation by:
1. Adjusting slide 1 (hook) to resonate with THIS audience's specific concerns
2. Identifying which slides are most relevant to them and should be expanded
3. Identifying which slides can be cut or summarized for this audience
4. Suggesting changes to language/terminology for their knowledge level
5. Adding or replacing 1 slide that is specifically valuable for this audience

What stays the same: [THE CORE MESSAGE / KEY DATA]
```

### 6.2 Standard Adaptations

| Original Audience | New Audience | Key Adaptation |
|------------------|-------------|----------------|
| Technical team | Executive board | Cut technical detail; lead with business impact; use financial language |
| Executive board | Frontline team | Replace strategy with operational implications; be personal and concrete |
| Internal team | External client | Remove internal context; add proof points; replace jargon with client-friendly language |
| Investor pitch | Customer demo | Replace financials with product benefits; replace projections with use cases |

## 7. Real-World Example: AI Presentations at BCG

**Company:** Boston Consulting Group (BCG) — global management consulting firm

**The Traditional Approach:**
Consultants at BCG spent 30–40% of project time building PowerPoint decks — transforming analysis into client-ready slides. For a 6-week engagement, this meant 2–3 weeks of PowerPoint creation alongside the analysis work.

**The AI Integration:**
BCG integrated AI (including a proprietary tool built on GPT-4) that could:
- Convert analyst notes and findings into a structured presentation outline
- Generate slide content from data summaries
- Suggest the right chart type for each data set
- Draft executive summaries and speaker notes

**The Result:**
- Presentation production time reduced by 50–60%
- Consultants redirected time to analysis depth and client interaction
- Slide quality improved in consistency (house style better enforced)
- Client feedback on presentation quality: unchanged or improved

**The lesson:** AI in presentations doesn't replace the consultant's insight or judgment — it removes the mechanical work of translating that insight into slides. The analysis, the storyline, and the recommendations remain human.

## 9. Interview Questions — Session 13

**Q1:** *"How do you use AI to create presentations?"*

**Strong Answer:**
"I use AI in three phases. First, structure: I give AI my topic, audience, goal, and raw content and ask it to generate a slide-by-slide outline using a narrative framework — usually Problem-Solution-Impact for business presentations. I review and adjust the narrative flow before drafting any content. Second, content: I generate slide-by-slide using AI, always specifying assertion-based titles — complete sentences that state the conclusion, not labels. I also generate speaker notes separately for each slide. Third, Q&A prep: I prompt AI to generate the 10 toughest questions a skeptical audience would ask, then prepare answers for the hardest 3–4. This approach builds a much more defensible, audience-aware presentation than a traditional blank-page approach."

## 11. Key Terminology — Session 13

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Narrative Arc** | The logical story structure that connects all slides into a coherent argument |
| **PSI Framework** | Problem-Solution-Impact — a proven presentation structure for business decks |
| **SCR Framework** | Situation-Complication-Resolution — McKinsey's executive briefing structure |
| **Assertion Title** | A slide title written as a complete sentence stating the key conclusion |
| **Label Title** | A weak slide title that names a topic without stating the insight |
