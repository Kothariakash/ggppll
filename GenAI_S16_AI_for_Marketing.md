```
TRADITIONAL MARKETING CONTENT WORKFLOW:
  Strategy → Brief → Copywriter drafts → Review cycles (2–4) → 
  Approval → Design → Publish
```

## 2. Establishing Brand Voice with AI

### 2.1 Why Brand Voice Matters in AI Content

Without brand voice definition, AI produces generic content that could belong to any company. With it, AI produces content that sounds authentically yours.

**The Brand Voice Definition Prompt:**
```
PROMPT:
Help me define our brand voice so AI can consistently produce on-brand content.

Our company: [NAME AND BRIEF DESCRIPTION]
Our customers: [WHO THEY ARE]
Our positioning: [HOW WE DIFFER FROM COMPETITORS]
Our values: [3–5 CORE VALUES]

Sample content I love (examples of our best content):
[PASTE 2–3 EXAMPLES OF YOUR BEST EXISTING CONTENT]

From these examples, define our brand voice across these dimensions:
1. PERSONALITY (3 adjectives that describe our brand's character)
2. TONE (formal/casual, warm/professional, serious/playful — be specific)
3. VOCABULARY (words we use, words we NEVER use)
4. SENTENCE STYLE (long/short, simple/complex, active/passive preference)
5. WHAT WE STAND FOR (the belief or point of view behind our brand)
6. WHAT WE AVOID (topics, language, claims we stay away from)

Format this as a Brand Voice Card I can paste into every AI content prompt.
```

**Example Brand Voice Card (for a sustainable fashion brand):**
```
BRAND VOICE CARD — EarthWeave Clothing
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PERSONALITY: Purposeful, warm, honest (never self-righteous)
TONE: Conversational and direct. We talk like a smart friend, not a 
      corporation. Professional but never stiff.
VOCABULARY: 
  USE: planet, material, crafted, honest, durable, story, community
  NEVER USE: luxury, premium, exclusive, groundbreaking, revolutionizing
SENTENCES: Short and punchy. Mix short sentences with occasional longer ones.
           Active voice. Direct address ("You" / "We").
BELIEF: People deserve to know what they're buying and who made it.
AVOID: Greenwashing language, vague sustainability claims, elitism
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

Once built, **prepend this card to every AI content prompt** for that brand.

## 3. Social Media Content at Scale

### 3.1 The Social Media Content Calendar

```
PROMPT:
You are a Social Media Strategist for [BRAND NAME].

[PASTE BRAND VOICE CARD]

Generate a 2-week social media content calendar for these platforms:
- Instagram (visual-first, Story + Feed)
- LinkedIn (professional, thought leadership)
- Twitter/X (conversational, newsy)

Our current campaign theme: [THEME — e.g., "Sustainable Living Month"]
Key product or message to feature: [PRODUCT/MESSAGE]
Target audience: [BRIEF AUDIENCE DESCRIPTION]

For each day, provide:
DATE | PLATFORM | CONTENT TYPE | TOPIC | HOOK/OPENING LINE | KEY MESSAGE

Format as a table. Monday–Friday for 2 weeks (10 posts per platform).
Include 2 "engagement" posts (questions, polls) per platform per week.
```

### 3.2 Instagram Caption Generator

```
PROMPT:
[PASTE BRAND VOICE CARD]

You are writing Instagram captions for [BRAND NAME].

Product/Topic being featured: [DESCRIBE PRODUCT OR TOPIC]
Visual being used: [DESCRIBE THE IMAGE OR VIDEO]
Campaign/moment: [SEASONAL, PRODUCT LAUNCH, MILESTONE, ETC.]

Generate 3 caption variations:

VARIATION A — STORYTELLING STYLE:
  2–3 short paragraphs. Tells a mini-story. Ends with a reflection or question.
  Hashtags: 8–12 relevant ones at the end.

VARIATION B — PUNCHY AND DIRECT:
  1–3 lines maximum. Bold opening. Clear benefit or claim. Strong CTA.
  Hashtags: 5–7 highly targeted ones.

VARIATION C — EDUCATIONAL / VALUE-ADD:
  Teaches the audience something. "3 things you didn't know about [X]" format.
  Ends with a CTA to save or share.
  Hashtags: 10–15 discovery-focused ones.

Do not use emojis unless specified. [OR: Use emojis sparingly — 1–2 per post]
```

### 3.3 LinkedIn Thought Leadership Post

```
PROMPT:
[PASTE BRAND VOICE CARD or PERSONAL VOICE]

Write a LinkedIn post for [YOUR NAME / BRAND] on this topic:
[TOPIC — your insight, experience, or perspective]

My personal experience or angle: [1–2 SENTENCES OF YOUR UNIQUE INSIGHT]
My credibility on this topic: [WHY SHOULD PEOPLE TRUST YOUR VIEW?]

Structure:
Line 1: Hook — a bold statement, surprising insight, or counterintuitive claim
[Line break]
Lines 2–5: The body — develop your insight with a specific example or data
[Line break]
Lines 6–8: 3–4 takeaways OR a short story OR a practical tip
[Line break]
Line 9–10: Close — question to invite comments OR a clear call to action

Rules:
- No more than 2 hashtags (place at the end)
- Line breaks between every 2–3 sentences
- Under 300 words
- No corporate buzzwords: "passionate," "humbled," "excited to share"
- Start with a statement, not "I" or "Today I want to talk about"
```

## 4. Blog Posts & Long-Form Content

### 4.1 The SEO Blog Post Workflow

A structured multi-prompt workflow for a complete, SEO-optimized blog post:

**STEP 1 — Keyword and Topic Research:**
```
PROMPT:
I want to write a blog post about [BROAD TOPIC].
My target audience: [DESCRIBE AUDIENCE]
My business/website: [BRIEF DESCRIPTION]

Help me:
1. Identify 5 specific long-tail keyword variations for this topic
   (e.g., not just "AI tools" but "best AI tools for small business marketing 2024")
2. For each keyword: estimated search intent (informational/commercial/navigational)
3. Suggest the strongest keyword combination to target
4. Recommend: what is the specific angle that would make this post
   MORE useful than the top Google results?
```

**STEP 2 — Outline Generation:**
```
PROMPT:
Write an SEO-optimized blog post outline for:
Title: [YOUR CHOSEN KEYWORD / TITLE]
Target keyword: [PRIMARY KEYWORD]
Word count target: [1,200 / 1,500 / 2,000 words]
Audience: [BRIEF DESCRIPTION]

Outline format:
H1: [FINAL TITLE — SEO-optimized]
INTRO: (hook + problem + promise — 100 words)
H2: [Section 1 title]
  H3: Sub-points
H2: [Section 2 title]
  H3: Sub-points
[Continue for all sections]
CONCLUSION: (summary + CTA)
META DESCRIPTION: Under 155 characters, includes primary keyword

Mark which sections benefit from: [DATA], [EXAMPLE], [VISUAL]
```

**STEP 3 — Section by Section Drafting:**
```
PROMPT:
Draft Section [X]: [SECTION TITLE] of this blog post.

Context: This post is about [TOPIC]. The target keyword is [KEYWORD].
Audience: [AUDIENCE — their knowledge level, what they care about]
Previous sections have covered: [BRIEF SUMMARY]

Requirements for this section:
- H2 heading that includes a keyword variation where natural
- 250–300 words
- At least 1 specific example or data point
- Conversational but authoritative tone
- No keyword stuffing — natural language
- End with a transition to the next section: [NEXT SECTION TITLE]

[PASTE ANY RELEVANT NOTES OR RESEARCH FOR THIS SECTION]
```

**STEP 4 — Introduction and Conclusion:**
```
PROMPT:
Write the introduction and conclusion for this blog post.

Title: [TITLE]
Primary keyword: [KEYWORD]
The post covers: [3-SENTENCE SUMMARY OF THE POST]
The reader's pain point: [WHAT PROBLEM DOES THIS POST SOLVE?]

INTRODUCTION requirements:
- Hook: Open with a surprising stat, bold claim, or vivid scenario (2 sentences)
- Problem: Acknowledge the reader's challenge (2 sentences)
- Promise: Tell them what they'll learn / be able to do after reading (1 sentence)
- Include primary keyword naturally in the first 100 words

CONCLUSION requirements:
- Summary: 2–3 sentences summarizing the key takeaways
- Application: Tell readers what to do FIRST (specific, actionable)
- CTA: 1 sentence with a clear next step (read X / download Y / comment below)
- End memorably — not with "In conclusion, as we have seen..."
```

## 5. Ad Copy & Email Marketing

### 5.1 Ad Copy Variations for A/B Testing

```
PROMPT:
[PASTE BRAND VOICE CARD]

Write 5 variations of ad copy for a [Google Search / Facebook / Instagram / LinkedIn] ad.

Product/Service: [WHAT YOU ARE ADVERTISING]
Target audience: [DESCRIBE SPECIFICALLY]
Main benefit: [THE #1 THING YOUR PRODUCT/SERVICE DOES FOR THEM]
Offer or hook: [DISCOUNT / TRIAL / UNIQUE FEATURE / GUARANTEE]
Primary competitor differentiator: [HOW YOU'RE BETTER OR DIFFERENT]

For each variation (label A–E):
- HEADLINE (under 30 characters for Google / 40 for Facebook)
- SUBHEADLINE (optional — under 90 characters)
- BODY COPY (under 125 characters for Facebook / 90 for Google)
- CTA BUTTON TEXT (under 20 characters — be specific, not just "Learn More")

TEST VARIABLES:
  A: Benefit-led headline
  B: Problem-led headline
  C: Social proof / number headline
  D: Question headline
  E: Urgency / scarcity headline

Do not repeat the same opening word in any headline.
```

### 5.2 Email Marketing Sequence

```
PROMPT:
[PASTE BRAND VOICE CARD]

Write a 5-email welcome sequence for new subscribers to [BRAND/NEWSLETTER].

Subscriber context: They signed up because [LEAD MAGNET / REASON].
What they want: [THEIR PRIMARY GOAL IN SUBSCRIBING]
What we want: [YOUR CONVERSION GOAL — purchase / trial / download]

EMAIL SEQUENCE:
Email 1 (Immediately after signup): Welcome + deliver the lead magnet + set expectations
Email 2 (Day 2): Share your most valuable piece of free content
Email 3 (Day 4): Tell your brand story / founding story
Email 4 (Day 6): Social proof — a customer success story
Email 5 (Day 8): Soft offer — introduce the product with a reason to try it now

For each email:
- Subject line (include a low-open-rate backup option)
- Preview text (under 90 characters)
- Email body (under 200 words — concise!)
- One clear CTA per email

Tone: [FROM BRAND VOICE CARD]. No pressure. Build trust first.
```

## 6. Content Repurposing — One Piece, Multiple Formats

### 6.1 The Repurposing Multiplier

One long-form piece of content can become 10+ pieces with AI assistance:

```
ONE BLOG POST (2,000 words) → AI REPURPOSING CREATES:

  LinkedIn article:    Same content, professional framing
  Twitter/X thread:    10 key points as a numbered thread
  Instagram carousel:  5 slides with key takeaways
  Email newsletter:    500-word summary with link to full post
  YouTube script:      Blog converted to spoken video script
  Podcast talking points: 6 discussion points with supporting details
  Infographic copy:    Stats and key facts extracted
  Short-form video script: 60-second version of the core idea
  Quote graphics:      3–5 quotable one-liners extracted
  FAQ post:            Q&A format targeting search intent
```

### 6.2 The Master Repurposing Prompt

```
PROMPT:
I have this piece of content: [PASTE YOUR BLOG POST / ARTICLE / TRANSCRIPT]

Repurpose it into the following formats. For each, adapt — do not just copy:

1. LINKEDIN POST (250 words, professional framing, assertion-based hook)
2. TWITTER THREAD (10 tweets, numbered, each under 280 chars, thread starter + 8 insights + call to action)
3. EMAIL NEWSLETTER EXCERPT (150 words, conversational, ends with link and CTA)
4. 5-SLIDE CAROUSEL OUTLINE (Slide 1: hook, Slides 2–4: key points, Slide 5: CTA)
5. 3 STAND-ALONE QUOTE GRAPHICS (each under 20 words — a powerful, shareable sentence)

[PASTE BRAND VOICE CARD]
Maintain voice consistency across all formats. Adapt for platform, not just copy-paste.
```

## 7. Real-World Example: Jasper AI at Scale

**Company:** A D2C beauty brand (100K monthly website visitors, 50K email subscribers)

**The Challenge:** The content team (2 people) needed to produce:
- 8 blog posts/month
- 50 social posts/month (Instagram + Facebook + Pinterest)
- 4 email campaigns/month
- 20 product descriptions/month

With 2 people, traditional production was falling behind. They tried adding freelancers but quality and brand consistency suffered.

**The AI Solution (Jasper AI + ChatGPT):**

```
IMPLEMENTATION:
  Week 1: Created Brand Voice Card in Jasper with 15 writing examples
  Week 2: Built prompt templates for each content type
  Week 3: Trained both team members on the workflow
  Week 4: First full AI-assisted content month

WORKFLOW:
  Content strategist: Creates briefs and topics (strategic work)
  AI (Jasper/ChatGPT): Generates first drafts for all content types
  Content creator: Reviews, edits, approves, schedules (execution)
```

**Results after 3 months:**

| Metric | Before AI | After AI | Change |
|--------|-----------|---------|--------|
| Blog posts/month | 4 | 12 | +200% |
| Social posts/month | 20 | 55 | +175% |
| Email campaigns/month | 2 | 4 | +100% |
| Product descriptions/month | 10 | 30 | +200% |
| Team size | 2 people | 2 people | Unchanged |
| Content quality score | 3.6/5 | 4.1/5 | +14% |

**Why quality improved:** With AI handling first drafts, the team spent their time on editing, strategy, and creative direction — higher-value work that directly improved content quality and brand consistency.

## 9. Interview Questions — Session 16

**Q1:** *"How would you use AI in a marketing role?"*

**Strong Answer:**
"My approach starts with codifying the brand voice — I build a Brand Voice Card from existing best-performing content so AI consistently produces on-brand output rather than generic text. Then I use AI across the content lifecycle: social media calendars, platform-specific captions, blog post drafting using a multi-step SEO workflow, email sequences, and ad copy variations for A/B testing. The highest-value use case is content repurposing — taking one well-researched long-form piece and converting it into 8–10 different formats using AI, which multiplies content velocity without multiplying headcount. I always verify facts, maintain brand standards, and treat AI output as a first draft that needs my strategic and creative judgment to become publish-ready."

## 11. Key Terminology — Session 16

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Brand Voice Card** | A documented definition of a brand's personality, tone, vocabulary, and writing style for use in AI prompts |
| **Content Calendar** | A scheduled plan of content topics, formats, and publication dates across platforms |
| **Content Repurposing** | Converting one piece of content into multiple formats for different platforms |
| **SEO-Optimized Content** | Content structured to rank on search engines by targeting specific search queries |
| **Long-Tail Keyword** | A specific, multi-word search phrase with lower volume but higher conversion intent |
| **A/B Testing (ad copy)** | Testing two or more variations of ad content to determine which performs better |
| **Welcome Sequence** | A series of automated emails sent to new subscribers over their first week |
| **Thought Leadership Post** | A social media post that shares an expert perspective to build professional credibility |
