## 1. AI's Role in Human Resources

### 1.1 The HR Time Allocation Problem

```
WHERE HR PROFESSIONALS SPEND THEIR TIME:
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Administrative tasks (documents, policies, forms):  40%
  Recruiting administration (JDs, screening, comms):  25%
  Performance management process:                     15%
  Employee relations and coaching:                    15%
  Strategic HR and culture:                            5%

WHAT HR PROFESSIONALS WISH THEY SPENT TIME ON:
  Strategic culture work:           30% (6x current allocation)
  Employee coaching and development: 30% (2x)
  Organizational design:             20%
  Administrative tasks:              10% (4x reduction desired)
  Recruiting administration:         10% (2.5x reduction desired)

AI CAN REALLOCATE: 35–45% of HR time from administrative to strategic
```

### 1.2 The Opportunity and the Responsibility

AI in HR offers significant efficiency gains, but also carries unique risks:

```
OPPORTUNITY:
  ✓ Eliminate blank-page paralysis on JDs, reviews, policies
  ✓ Standardize process quality across a large HR team
  ✓ Faster documentation → more time for human interactions
  ✓ Consistent structure in performance feedback

RESPONSIBILITY:
  ⚠ AI can perpetuate hiring bias from biased training data
  ⚠ Performance review AI can reflect historical biases
  ⚠ Policy AI may miss jurisdiction-specific legal requirements
  ⚠ AI must NEVER replace human judgment in hiring or firing decisions
  ⚠ Candidate data must never be entered into public AI tools
```

**The Golden Rule for AI in HR:** AI assists with documents and structure. Humans make all people decisions.

## 2. Job Descriptions — Writing and Bias Auditing

### 2.1 The Inclusive Job Description Generator

```
PROMPT:
You are a Talent Acquisition Specialist with expertise in inclusive hiring practices.

Write a job description for the following role. Make it engaging, specific,
and inclusive — it should attract a diverse, qualified candidate pool.

Role: [JOB TITLE]
Department: [DEPARTMENT]
Level: [Individual Contributor / Manager / Director / VP]
Location: [CITY / REMOTE / HYBRID]
Key responsibilities: [LIST 4–6 CORE RESPONSIBILITIES]
Must-have qualifications: [LIST 3–4 GENUINE REQUIREMENTS]
Nice-to-have qualifications: [LIST 2–3 PREFERENCES — clearly labeled as preferred]
Culture highlights: [2–3 GENUINE CULTURE POINTS]

Structure:
1. ROLE HEADLINE (1 sentence — make it exciting and specific)
2. ABOUT THE OPPORTUNITY (2–3 sentences — impact, team, growth potential)
3. WHAT YOU'LL DO (5–7 bullets — present-tense verbs, specific outcomes)
4. WHAT YOU BRING (4–5 bullets — must-haves only, each genuinely required)
5. WHAT WE OFFER (3–5 bullets — genuine benefits and culture points)
6. ABOUT US (2–3 sentences — mission, values, diversity statement)

Inclusion rules:
- Avoid masculine-coded language: "ninja," "rockstar," "dominate," "aggressive"
- Replace "years of experience" with "demonstrated ability to..."
- Replace degree requirements with skill requirements unless legally necessary
- Use gender-neutral language throughout
- Do not imply age, physical requirements unless genuinely job-relevant
```

### 2.2 The Bias Audit Prompt

Run every job description through this before posting:

```
PROMPT:
You are an inclusion and diversity specialist. Audit this job description
for language that may discourage qualified candidates from applying.

Check for:
1. MASCULINE-CODED LANGUAGE (words that research shows discourage women applicants)
   Examples: competitive, driven, aggressive, dominant, ninja, rockstar, crusade
2. AGE-CODED LANGUAGE (implying preference for younger/older workers)
   Examples: "digital native," "recent graduate," "energy and stamina"
3. UNNECESSARY REQUIREMENTS (qualifications that screen out diverse candidates)
   Examples: degrees for roles that don't need them, overly specific "X years"
4. CULTURAL-FIT TRAPS (vague culture language that may mean "like us")
   Examples: "culture fit," "work hard play hard," "family atmosphere"
5. EXCLUSIONARY BENEFITS FRAMING (assumes a particular life situation)
   Examples: "ping pong table," "beer Fridays" (excludes non-drinkers)

For each issue found:
- Quote the specific text
- Explain why it may be exclusionary
- Provide a more inclusive alternative

JOB DESCRIPTION:
[PASTE JD HERE]
```

## 3. Interview Framework Design

### 3.1 Structured Interview Question Bank

Structured interviews (where all candidates answer the same questions) are significantly more predictive of job performance than unstructured interviews. AI makes building them fast.

```
PROMPT:
You are an I/O Psychologist and talent assessment specialist.

Design a structured interview framework for: [JOB TITLE]
Interview type: [COMPETENCY-BASED / BEHAVIORAL / SITUATIONAL / HYBRID]
Interview duration: [30 / 45 / 60 minutes]

Core competencies to assess (choose 4–5 from this list or specify your own):
  Communication | Problem-solving | Collaboration | Leadership |
  Adaptability | Analytical thinking | Customer focus | Ownership |
  [YOUR OWN COMPETENCY]

For each competency, generate:
1. BEHAVIORAL QUESTION (past behavior predicts future performance)
   Format: "Tell me about a time when you..."
2. SITUATIONAL QUESTION (hypothetical scenario)
   Format: "Imagine you are in a situation where..."
3. FOLLOW-UP PROBE (to get beyond surface answers)
   Format: "What specifically did YOU do?" / "What was the outcome?"
4. GREEN FLAG (what a strong answer sounds like)
5. RED FLAG (what a weak or concerning answer sounds like)

Also provide:
- One "culture fit" question that can be asked consistently (not vague)
- One question that allows the candidate to showcase something unique
- A closing question: "What questions do you have for us?"
  (note: this is always the same — give 3 good answers to watch for)
```

### 3.2 Interview Scoring Rubric Generator

```
PROMPT:
Create a structured scoring rubric for the interview questions I will use to
assess candidates for [JOB TITLE].

For each of these competencies: [LIST YOUR COMPETENCIES]
Generate a scoring guide:

COMPETENCY: [NAME]
1 — DOES NOT MEET: [What a 1-point answer looks like — specific behaviors]
2 — PARTIALLY MEETS: [What a 2-point answer looks like]
3 — MEETS EXPECTATIONS: [What a 3-point answer looks like — this is the bar]
4 — EXCEEDS: [What a 4-point answer looks like]
5 — EXCEPTIONAL: [What a 5-point answer looks like — rare but recognizable]

Also include:
- A note on what interviewers should avoid (bias traps in scoring)
- A recommended debrief format for the hiring panel
```

## 4. Performance Reviews

### 4.1 The Performance Review Challenge

```
THE PROBLEM WITH TRADITIONAL PERFORMANCE REVIEWS:
  ▸ Managers write vague comments: "Good team player," "Meets expectations"
  ▸ Comments reflect recency bias (only remember last 2 months)
  ▸ Inconsistent language across managers — unfair to employees
  ▸ Development-focus is superficial or missing entirely
  ▸ Average manager spends 3–5 hours per review cycle per direct report

AI SOLUTION:
  ▸ Convert bullet notes into specific, structured narrative
  ▸ Apply consistent structure across all managers
  ▸ Ensure development focus is present in every review
  ▸ Reduce time: 3–5 hours → 45 minutes (with better outcomes)
```

### 4.2 Performance Review Narrative Generator

```
PROMPT:
You are an experienced HR Business Partner helping a manager write
performance review comments that are specific, fair, and developmental.

Employee context:
  Role: [JOB TITLE]
  Level: [Junior / Mid / Senior / Manager]
  Tenure in role: [X months/years]
  Overall rating: [Exceptional / Exceeds / Meets / Partially Meets / Does Not Meet]

Manager's raw notes about this employee:
[PASTE YOUR BULLET POINTS — achievements, examples, areas for growth]

Write a structured performance review narrative:

SECTION 1 — OVERALL PERFORMANCE SUMMARY (2–3 sentences)
  Open with a clear performance statement. Mention rating implication.
  
SECTION 2 — KEY STRENGTHS (2–3 specific examples)
  For each strength:
  - Name the behavior/competency
  - Provide the specific example from my notes
  - State the business impact (what did this achieve?)

SECTION 3 — DEVELOPMENT AREAS (1–2 areas)
  For each area:
  - Name the specific gap (not vague: not "communication" but "upward communication")
  - Frame developmentally: "An area for continued growth is..."
  - Provide a specific observable example
  - Suggest one concrete development action

SECTION 4 — GOALS FOR NEXT PERIOD (2–3 goals)
  Each goal: SMART format (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)

Rules:
- Specific examples only — no vague phrases like "good team player"
- Active voice
- No surprises — only what has been discussed during the year
- Under 500 words total
- Do not write anything that could not be said directly to the employee
```

### 4.3 360-Degree Feedback Synthesis

When you have feedback from multiple sources, AI synthesizes it:

```
PROMPT:
I have collected 360-degree feedback for [EMPLOYEE NAME/ROLE].
Here is the raw feedback from multiple reviewers (anonymized):

PEER FEEDBACK:
[PASTE ANONYMIZED PEER COMMENTS]

MANAGER FEEDBACK:
[PASTE MANAGER COMMENTS]

DIRECT REPORT FEEDBACK (if applicable):
[PASTE DIRECT REPORT COMMENTS]

SELF-ASSESSMENT:
[PASTE EMPLOYEE'S SELF-COMMENTS]

Synthesize this feedback into a balanced, actionable summary:
1. THEMES OF STRENGTH: What do multiple reviewers consistently praise?
2. THEMES OF DEVELOPMENT: What development areas appear across multiple sources?
3. PERCEPTION GAPS: Where does the employee's self-assessment differ significantly
   from others' perceptions? (This requires careful handling)
4. TOP 2 DEVELOPMENT PRIORITIES: Most important areas based on frequency and impact
5. SUGGESTED CONVERSATION STARTERS: 2–3 questions the manager can use in the
   feedback conversation to make it a dialogue, not a monologue

Maintain confidentiality: Do not attribute any specific comment to an individual.
Tone: Developmental and forward-looking. Not evaluative or harsh.
```

## 6. HR Policy Drafting

### 6.1 Policy Document Prompt

```
PROMPT:
You are an HR Policy Specialist.

Draft a [POLICY NAME] policy for [COMPANY TYPE — e.g., 200-person technology company].

Policy scope: [WHO THIS APPLIES TO]
Key requirements we want to enforce: [BULLET LIST YOUR KEY RULES]
Our company values relevant here: [1–2 VALUES]
Special considerations: [ANY SPECIFIC CIRCUMSTANCES — e.g., remote teams, multi-state]

Policy structure:
1. PURPOSE (why this policy exists — 1 paragraph)
2. SCOPE (who it covers — specific and inclusive)
3. POLICY STATEMENT (the core rules — numbered, clear, unambiguous)
4. EMPLOYEE RESPONSIBILITIES (what employees must do)
5. MANAGER RESPONSIBILITIES (what managers must do)
6. PROCEDURE (how to handle situations covered by this policy)
7. VIOLATIONS AND CONSEQUENCES (clearly stated)
8. EXCEPTIONS PROCESS (how to request exceptions)
9. RELATED POLICIES (cross-references)
10. POLICY OWNER AND REVIEW DATE

Important:
- Flag any section requiring legal review as [REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW]
- Flag any jurisdiction-specific requirement as [JURISDICTION-SPECIFIC]
- Flag any section needing current labor law verification as [VERIFY CURRENT LAW]
- Do not present any specific legal requirements as definitive without verification

After the policy, provide a 3-sentence plain-English summary that could be
used in a team communication announcing the policy.
```

## 7. Responsible AI in HR — Critical Safeguards

### 7.1 Non-Negotiable Rules for AI in HR

```
RULE 1: NEVER INPUT IDENTIFIABLE CANDIDATE/EMPLOYEE DATA
  ✗ Do NOT paste: full names, employee IDs, specific performance ratings,
    compensation data, medical information, or any PII into public AI tools
  ✓ Anonymize all data: use "Employee A," "Candidate X," "Senior Manager role"

RULE 2: AI SCREENS PROCESSES, NOT PEOPLE
  ✗ Do NOT use AI to screen or rank resumes automatically
    (high risk of perpetuating historical hiring biases)
  ✓ Use AI to improve the process (better JDs, better questions)
    not to replace human review of individuals

RULE 3: PERFORMANCE DECISIONS STAY WITH HUMANS
  ✗ Do NOT use AI output as the sole basis for rating, promoting,
    or terminating employees
  ✓ Use AI to draft narratives, then human manager reviews and owns them

RULE 4: LEGAL REVIEW IS MANDATORY
  AI does not know your jurisdiction's current employment law.
  All AI-drafted policies must be reviewed by qualified legal counsel
  before implementation.

RULE 5: TRANSPARENCY WITH EMPLOYEES
  If AI tools are used in hiring processes, consider disclosure
  to candidates — some jurisdictions legally require it.
```

### 7.2 The HR AI Ethics Checklist

```
Before using AI in any HR task, confirm:
☐ No personally identifiable information entered into the prompt
☐ AI output will be reviewed by a human before use
☐ The decision (hire/fire/rate/promote) will be made by a human, not AI
☐ AI draft has been checked for biased language (bias audit run)
☐ Legal/compliance team has reviewed any AI-drafted policies
☐ Documentation: note that AI assisted in draft creation (internal record)
```

## 8. Real-World Example: Unilever's AI-Assisted Hiring

**Company:** Unilever — global consumer goods company, 148,000+ employees

**The Challenge:** Unilever receives 1.8 million job applications annually. Processing them manually was creating:
- Long delays (weeks before candidates heard back)
- Inconsistent screening quality
- Exhausted recruiters spending time on process, not relationships
- High cost per hire

**The AI Implementation:**
Unilever deployed an AI-assisted screening process (using HireVue and machine learning) for entry-level roles:

```
STAGE 1: Application + online games (AI-analyzed)
  AI assessed: cognitive ability, problem-solving, personality indicators
  NOT: name, age, school, photo — blind screening

STAGE 2: Video interview (AI-analyzed)
  Candidate answers standardized questions
  AI analyzed: language, tone, competency indicators
  NOT replacing human judgment — flagging for human review

STAGE 3: Human review of AI-shortlisted candidates
  Recruiters reviewed AI recommendations and made final decisions
  Humans held final screening authority in all cases

STAGE 4: Assessment centre (fully human)
  Shortlisted candidates invited for in-person assessment
```

**Results:**
- Time-to-hire reduced from 4 months to 4 weeks
- Cost per hire reduced by 16%
- Diversity: increased proportion of first-generation university students hired
- Recruiter time on value-add activities increased significantly
- Candidate satisfaction scores improved (faster process, clearer feedback)

**The Lesson:** AI handled the volume problem. Humans handled the relationship and judgment. The combination was better than either alone — but required careful design to prevent bias amplification.

**Unilever's caution:** They continuously monitor for bias in AI outputs, run regular audits, and have a human oversight board reviewing the AI's recommendations at aggregate level.

## 10. Interview Questions — Session 17

**Q1:** *"How would you use AI responsibly in an HR function?"*

**Strong Answer:**
"The key principle is that AI handles documents and process structure — humans handle all people decisions. Practically, I use AI to write job descriptions and then run a bias audit prompt to flag exclusionary language before posting. For interviews, AI helps me build structured question banks with behavioral and situational questions and scoring rubrics — which research shows are significantly more predictive than unstructured interviews. For performance reviews, I give AI my bullet-note observations and it produces a structured, specific narrative that I then review, own, and customize. The non-negotiables: I never input identifiable employee data into public AI tools, I never use AI output as the basis for a hiring or termination decision without human review, and I flag all AI-drafted policies for legal review before implementation."

## 12. Key Terminology — Session 17

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Structured Interview** | An interview where all candidates answer the same standardized questions, scored consistently |
| **Behavioral Question** | Interview question asking about past behavior ("Tell me about a time when...") |
| **Situational Question** | Interview question presenting a hypothetical scenario to assess judgment |
| **360-Degree Feedback** | Performance input collected from an employee's manager, peers, and direct reports |
| **Bias Audit** | A systematic review of a document (JD, review, policy) for language that may disadvantage certain groups |
| **SMART Goal** | Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound — a performance goal framework |
| **30-60-90 Day Plan** | A structured onboarding roadmap for a new employee's first three months |
| **PII (Personally Identifiable Information)** | Data that can identify a specific individual — must not be entered into public AI tools |
