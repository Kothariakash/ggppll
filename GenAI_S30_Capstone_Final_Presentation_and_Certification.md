## 2. Final Presentation Execution Guide

### 2.1 The 10-Minute Delivery Sequence

Use this as your mental script for the day:

```
[SLIDE 1 — 0:00 to 0:20]
YOUR OPENING HOOK:
  Do NOT say "Hi, my name is..."
  Open directly with your chosen hook format (Statistic / Scenario / Question).
  Make eye contact. Pause after the hook lands.
  Then: "I'm [NAME], and I built a solution that addresses exactly this."

[SLIDE 2 — 0:20 to 1:20]
THE PROBLEM:
  Make the audience feel the pain before you show the cure.
  Use your assertion title to state the impact.
  Reference the specific person who has this problem.
  State the quantified impact — the numbers you validated.
  End with a bridge: "The reason this problem persists is what I want to talk about next."

[SLIDE 3 — 1:20 to 2:05]
THE ROOT CAUSE:
  This is your insight slide — the "a-ha" moment.
  State the root cause clearly in one sentence.
  Contrast it with what others typically try (and why it's insufficient).
  Bridge: "Understanding this, I designed a solution that addresses the root cause — not the symptom."

[SLIDE 4 — 2:05 to 2:35]
THE SOLUTION:
  State it in 2–3 sentences. Then stop.
  Resist the urge to explain everything here — Slide 5 does that.
  Bridge: "Let me show you exactly how it works."

[SLIDE 5 — 2:35 to 3:35]
HOW IT WORKS:
  Walk through your pipeline — one step at a time.
  Keep it simple: Input → [Steps] → Output.
  Reference the AI tools by name — shows your technical credibility.
  Bridge: "The best way to understand this is to see it live."

[SLIDE 6 — 3:35 to 6:35]
LIVE DEMONSTRATION (3 minutes):
  Follow your demo script exactly.
  Show the input first, explain it briefly.
  Run the demo — narrate what's happening as it generates.
  Walk through the output — highlight the 3 most impressive elements.
  End the demo before time — rushing is worse than pausing.
  Bridge: "Let me show you what this means in terms of results."

[SLIDE 7 — 6:35 to 7:35]
RESULTS AND EVIDENCE:
  State each metric assertion-first: "Review time: 45 minutes to 14 minutes."
  Reference your testing methodology briefly: "Validated across 5 timed test sessions."
  Present the before/after side-by-side on this slide.
  Bridge: "Which translates to this business impact."

[SLIDE 8 — 7:35 to 8:05]
BUSINESS IMPACT:
  State the extrapolated value: hours saved × frequency × team size.
  Convert to money if possible. Round numbers are fine.
  Note the responsible AI safeguards built in — shows professional maturity.

[SLIDE 9 — 8:05 to 8:35]
LIMITATIONS AND NEXT STEPS:
  Name your limitations before the evaluators do.
  This is a strength — it shows you know your solution completely.
  Then pivot to next steps: "These are the three extensions I would build next."

[SLIDE 10 — 8:35 to 9:00]
SUMMARY AND CLOSE:
  State your 3 takeaways — fast, crisp, confident.
  Close with your pre-planned final line.
  "The solution is available for your review. I'm happy to take questions."

[0:25 buffer for transitions and natural pauses]
```

### 2.2 Managing the Live Demo

The demonstration is the highest-risk, highest-reward moment of your presentation.

```
BEFORE RUNNING THE DEMO:
  ✓ Input is pre-typed and ready to paste (don't type live — paste)
  ✓ Browser tab is on the correct page
  ✓ Pre-run output document is open in a separate tab (your backup)
  ✓ You know exactly what you'll say while the AI is generating

DURING THE DEMO — NARRATION GUIDE:
  While pasting input: "I'm going to give the system this input — [describe it briefly]"
  While AI is generating: "While this is running, note that the system is... [explain one design feature]"
  After output appears: "Here's what was produced. Notice these three things..."

IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG:
  AI tool slow or down: "Let me show you a pre-run output that I produced during testing."
  [Switch to your backup tab — do this without apology or panic]
  "This is the exact type of output the system produces — same quality as you'd see live."

THE GOLDEN RULE OF DEMOS:
  The evaluators are not testing whether ChatGPT can generate in real time.
  They are testing whether your SOLUTION works and whether YOU built something valuable.
  A pre-run backup demonstrated confidently is MORE impressive than a panicked live attempt.
```

## 3. Q&A Mastery

### 3.1 The 5 Universal Evaluator Questions

Every evaluator, regardless of domain expertise, typically asks versions of these questions. Know them cold:

**Q1: "What happens if the AI gives a wrong or hallucinated output?"**
```
ANSWER STRUCTURE:
DIRECT: "That's exactly why I built a human review checkpoint before any output is used."
EVIDENCE: "In 15+ test runs, I identified [X] instances of the AI flagging gaps as 
[DATA NEEDED] rather than inventing information — because that constraint is built 
into the prompt. I also tested with adversarial inputs specifically designed to 
cause hallucination."
ACKNOWLEDGMENT: "Hallucination is a genuine risk in any AI system, and any 
professional deployment needs to account for it."
FORWARD VIEW: "At scale, I'd add a quality sampling check — reviewing 10% of 
outputs weekly — and iterate the prompt based on any failures found."
```

**Q2: "How does this scale beyond your test cases?"**
```
ANSWER STRUCTURE:
DIRECT: "The solution is designed to be scalable — it works the same way whether 
5 or 500 people use it."
EVIDENCE: "The Custom GPT / prompt system requires no technical setup for new users 
— they follow the Quick Start Guide and are productive in under 10 minutes."
ACKNOWLEDGMENT: "Scale does introduce more edge cases I haven't tested for yet."
FORWARD VIEW: "At organizational scale, I'd add a prompt manager role who handles 
refinement based on aggregated feedback, and potentially a Zapier automation for 
high-volume use cases."
```

**Q3: "What data privacy concerns does this raise?"**
```
ANSWER STRUCTURE:
DIRECT: "Data privacy was a first-order design constraint, not an afterthought."
EVIDENCE: "The solution explicitly instructs users not to input personally identifiable 
information. All my tests used anonymized or fictional data. For enterprise deployment, 
I'd recommend using Azure OpenAI or Copilot Enterprise, which process data under 
enterprise data protection agreements."
ACKNOWLEDGMENT: "For any solution that handles real employee or customer data, 
enterprise-grade data governance is non-negotiable."
FORWARD VIEW: "The full deployment roadmap includes IT governance review and 
potential migration to an enterprise AI platform."
```

**Q4: "How do you know this is better than just using ChatGPT directly?"**
```
ANSWER STRUCTURE:
DIRECT: "The difference is in the system design — not just the prompts themselves."
EVIDENCE: "I ran a direct comparison: the same inputs to ChatGPT with no prompt 
context vs. my structured solution. The average quality score went from [X]/25 to 
[Y]/25 — a [Z]-point improvement. More importantly, the unstructured ChatGPT 
approach took [time] and required expertise to prompt well. My solution takes 
[less time] and requires no prompting knowledge from the user."
ACKNOWLEDGMENT: "Raw ChatGPT with a good prompt can produce similar outputs — 
the value of a designed system is consistency, repeatability, and accessibility 
for non-expert users."
FORWARD VIEW: "As the solution matures, I'd add more automated quality checking 
that further widens the quality gap."
```

**Q5: "What would you do differently if you were to build this again?"**
```
ANSWER STRUCTURE:
DIRECT: "Three things."
EVIDENCE: "[1]: I would conduct user testing earlier — ideally before building, 
not after. I found usability issues in Session 28 that would have shaped my design 
decisions if I'd known about them earlier.
[2]: I would invest more time in stress testing edge cases from the start 
my prompt needed two revision cycles to handle incomplete inputs gracefully.
[3]: I would define the success metrics more precisely before building my 
initial metric of 'saves time' was too vague; the final '67% reduction' 
only emerged because I forced myself to measure it."
ACKNOWLEDGMENT: "These are the exact lessons that turn a first project into 
a stronger second one."
FORWARD VIEW: "All three inform how I'd approach my next AI solution design."
```
