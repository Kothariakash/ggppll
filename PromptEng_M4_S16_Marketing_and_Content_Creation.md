## 16.1 AI's Impact on Marketing

Marketing is among the highest-value domains for AI-assisted prompt engineering:

- **High volume:** A single brand needs content across 5–10 platforms simultaneously
- **Brand consistency:** Every piece must sound like the brand, not the writer
- **Speed demands:** Content calendars run 30–90 days ahead
- **A/B testing:** Requires multiple variations of every asset
- **Repurposing:** One piece of cornerstone content should fuel 10+ derivative pieces

Well-prompted AI can reduce content production time by 60–80% while maintaining or improving quality — freeing marketing professionals to focus on strategy, audience insight, and creative direction.

## 16.2 Brand Voice Establishment

Before generating any marketing content, you must establish brand voice. This is the most critical foundation.

### Brand Voice Discovery Prompt

```
You are a brand strategist who specializes in defining and codifying brand voice
for growth-stage companies.

Analyze the following brand samples and define our brand voice:

SAMPLE 1: [Paste existing brand copy — website hero text, email, social post]
SAMPLE 2: [Paste another sample]
SAMPLE 3: [Paste another sample]

Define our brand voice across these dimensions:
1. PERSONALITY (3–5 adjectives that describe our brand's character)
2. TONE SPECTRUM (where do we sit on each axis?)
   - Formal ←→ Casual
   - Serious ←→ Playful
   - Technical ←→ Simple
   - Reserved ←→ Bold
3. VOCABULARY RULES (words we love / words we never use)
4. SENTENCE STYLE (length preference, structure patterns, punctuation style)
5. BRAND PROMISES (recurring themes and values we always express)
6. WHAT WE ARE NOT (explicit anti-patterns to avoid)

Then write a 50-word brand voice statement that captures our essence.
This becomes the foundation for all content prompts going forward.
```

### Brand Voice Template (Once Established)

```
BRAND VOICE SUMMARY FOR PROMPTS:
Our brand voice is: [3 adjectives]
We sound like: [short analogy — e.g., "a knowledgeable friend, not a salesperson"]
We always: [2–3 key stylistic commitments]
We never: [2–3 explicit exclusions]
Example of our voice: "[Paste a best-in-class example sentence]"
```

## 16.3 Social Media Content

### Instagram — Caption + Hashtags

```
Write 3 Instagram post captions for [BRAND NAME], a [TYPE OF BUSINESS].

Brand voice: [Paste your brand voice summary]
Product/topic: [WHAT THIS POST IS ABOUT]
Campaign theme: [OVERARCHING CAMPAIGN IF APPLICABLE]
Target audience: [DESCRIBE — demographics + what they care about]

Each caption must:
- Hook in the first 1–2 lines (before the "more" cutoff — under 125 characters for hook)
- Deliver the key message in the body (2–4 sentences)
- End with a clear, specific CTA
- Include 8–12 relevant hashtags (mix: 3 broad, 3 medium, 3 niche)
- Be under 150 words (body only, before hashtags)

Three style variations:
Caption A: Inspirational/aspirational tone
Caption B: Educational/value-led tone
Caption C: Storytelling/human tone

Label each clearly. Do not repeat vocabulary between captions.
```

### LinkedIn — Thought Leadership

```
Write a LinkedIn thought leadership post for [AUTHOR NAME], [THEIR ROLE] at [COMPANY].

Author's perspective: [What is their unique professional insight or experience?]
Topic: [SPECIFIC INSIGHT OR OBSERVATION FROM THEIR WORK]
Target audience: [WHO FOLLOWS THEM — professionals in what field?]
Core message: [The one takeaway the reader should have]

LinkedIn post structure:
- HOOK (line 1 only — bold, 1 punchy sentence — under 15 words)
- LINE BREAK
- CONTEXT (1–2 sentences expanding the hook — why this matters)
- INSIGHT (3–5 short paragraphs or bullets with the actual meat)
- PERSONAL ELEMENT (1–2 sentences connecting this to author's experience)
- CLOSING QUESTION (1 question that invites comments — specific, not generic)
- 3–5 hashtags

Tone: Authoritative but human. Professional but not stiff.
First person ("I" voice). No buzzwords.
Length: 200–280 words (body only, excluding hashtags).
```

### Twitter/X Thread

```
Write a Twitter/X thread on [TOPIC] for [BRAND/AUTHOR].

Target audience: [DESCRIBE]
Core argument: [What is the thread's thesis?]
Style: [Educational / Controversial / Story-driven / List-based]

Thread structure:
Tweet 1 (HOOK): Bold claim or compelling question — makes people stop scrolling.
                Under 220 characters. Must work as a standalone tweet.
Tweets 2–7 (BODY): One distinct point per tweet. Each must:
                    - Start with a number or strong transition word
                    - Be self-contained (understandable without reading others)
                    - Include a concrete example or statistic where possible
                    - Under 250 characters
Tweet 8 (SUMMARY): Distill the thread's lesson in 1–2 sentences
Tweet 9 (CTA): What should people do? Follow / Save / Share / Comment / Link

Rules:
- No tweet longer than 270 characters
- Each tweet must be valuable on its own — not a cliffhanger
- Vary tweet length and structure for readability
- 2–3 emojis maximum per tweet (only where they add meaning)
```

### Email Newsletter

```
Write an email newsletter for [BRAND] sent to [AUDIENCE DESCRIPTION].

Newsletter name: [IF APPLICABLE]
This edition's theme: [THEME OR TOPIC]
Tone: [BRAND VOICE — conversational / educational / inspirational]
Send frequency: [Weekly / Bi-weekly / Monthly]

Structure:
SUBJECT LINE: [2 options — one curiosity, one benefit-led]
PREVIEW TEXT: [Under 90 characters — expands the subject line]

SECTION 1 — OPENER (100 words):
  Personal, conversational note from the brand. Something real, not generic.
  Reference something timely or audience-relevant.

SECTION 2 — MAIN CONTENT (200–300 words):
  Core educational or valuable content for this week's theme.
  Subheadline + 3–4 short paragraphs or bullets.

SECTION 3 — FEATURED RESOURCE OR CTA (50–75 words):
  One clear recommendation or action (article, product, event, offer).
  Button text suggestion included.

SECTION 4 — CLOSING (30–50 words):
  Warm, on-brand sign-off.

Total under 500 words. Mobile-first: short paragraphs, plenty of white space.
```

## 16.4 Blog & Long-Form Content

### SEO Blog Post — Full Production Workflow

**Step 1 — Keyword Brief:**
```
Generate an SEO content brief for a blog post targeting the keyword:
Primary keyword: [KEYWORD]
Secondary keywords: [2–3 related terms]
Target audience: [DESCRIBE]
Search intent: [Informational / Commercial / Transactional]

Brief should include:
1. Recommended title (60 characters max, contains primary keyword)
2. Meta description (155 characters max, contains keyword, has CTA)
3. H2 structure (5–7 sections) — each H2 should target a secondary keyword or question
4. H3 sub-sections for the 2 most detailed sections
5. Word count recommendation
6. Content type (how-to / listicle / pillar / opinion / case study)
7. Tone and audience calibration
8. 3 internal link opportunities (topics to reference)
9. Competitor gap: what do top-ranking articles NOT cover that ours should?
```

**Step 2 — Introduction:**
```
Write the introduction for a blog post on: [TOPIC]
Primary keyword: [KEYWORD]
Target reader: [DESCRIBE]
Their pain point this article solves: [DESCRIBE]

Introduction rules:
- Hook in first sentence (question, statistic, bold claim, or scenario)
- Acknowledge the pain point (empathize — show you understand their problem)
- Promise (what will they know/be able to do after reading?)
- Primary keyword in first 100 words (naturally, not forced)
- Under 150 words total
- No "In this article, we will..." opener
```

**Step 3 — Section Writing:**
```
Write the [SECTION NAME] section of my blog post about [TOPIC].

Section heading (H2): [HEADING]
Word count target: [150–250 words]
Key points to cover: [LIST 3–4 POINTS]
Examples to include: [ANY SPECIFIC EXAMPLES]
Keywords to weave in naturally: [SECONDARY KEYWORDS]

Style:
- 1 clear idea per paragraph
- Short paragraphs (3–4 sentences max)
- Include one actionable tip or concrete example
- End with a transition to [NEXT SECTION TOPIC]
```

**Step 4 — CTA and Meta:**
```
Complete the blog post with:

1. CONCLUSION (100 words):
   - Summarize the 3 most important takeaways
   - Reinforce the transformation promised in the intro
   - CTA: specific next step (not generic "learn more")

2. META TITLE (60 chars max, keyword first):
3. META DESCRIPTION (155 chars max, keyword + CTA + benefit):
4. SLUG (URL — lowercase, hyphens, under 60 chars):
5. SOCIAL SHARING HEADLINE (for Facebook/LinkedIn — 80 chars max):
6. ALT TEXT for 3 suggested images:
```

## 16.5 Ad Copy & Conversion Copywriting

### PPC Ad Copy (Google Ads)

```
Write Google Search Ad copy for:
Product/Service: [DESCRIBE]
Target keyword: [PRIMARY KEYWORD]
Key differentiator: [WHY US vs. COMPETITORS]
Offer or CTA: [WHAT ACTION, WHAT INCENTIVE]

Generate:
3 Responsive Search Ad variations, each containing:
- Headline 1 (30 chars max): [Primary keyword inclusion]
- Headline 2 (30 chars max): [Key benefit or differentiator]
- Headline 3 (30 chars max): [CTA or trust signal]
- Description 1 (90 chars max): [Expand benefit + specific detail]
- Description 2 (90 chars max): [Social proof + urgency/offer]

Variations:
Version A: Benefit-led (lead with outcome for the user)
Version B: Problem-led (lead with the pain they're solving)
Version C: Trust-led (lead with proof point or credential)

Label each clearly. Character counts shown in [brackets] after each line.
```

### Landing Page Copy

```
Write conversion-optimized landing page copy for:
Product: [NAME AND 1-SENTENCE DESCRIPTION]
Target audience: [WHO IS THIS PAGE FOR]
Their primary pain point: [WHAT PROBLEM DO THEY HAVE]
Our primary solution: [HOW WE SOLVE IT]
Offer: [WHAT ARE WE ASKING THEM TO DO — free trial, book a call, buy now]
Key social proof: [TESTIMONIALS, STATS, LOGOS IF ANY]

Landing page sections:
1. HERO SECTION:
   - Headline (10 words max): Focus on outcome, not product
   - Sub-headline (20 words max): Clarify who this is for and how
   - CTA button text (3–5 words): Action + outcome
   - Trust signal (1 line below CTA): [e.g., "Trusted by 1,200+ teams"]

2. PROBLEM SECTION (100 words):
   Agitate the pain. Make the reader feel understood.

3. SOLUTION SECTION (150 words):
   Present our solution as the natural answer. Benefits not features.

4. SOCIAL PROOF SECTION:
   - 3 testimonial templates (structure: RESULT + SITUATION + RECOMMENDATION)
   - Stats section (3 numbers with labels)

5. CTA SECTION (50 words):
   Final push. Repeat the core promise. Low-friction ask. Remove objections.

Copywriting rules: "You" language throughout. Benefits before features.
Specificity over generality. One CTA repeated, not multiple different CTAs.
```

## 16.6 A/B Testing with AI

```
I need to A/B test this email subject line:
Original: [CURRENT SUBJECT LINE]

The email is about: [DESCRIBE]
Current open rate: [IF KNOWN]
Audience: [DESCRIBE]

Generate 8 test variations covering these angles:
1. Curiosity gap (makes them wonder what's inside)
2. Benefit-first (leads with the value they'll get)
3. Urgency (time or scarcity element)
4. Social proof (what others are doing)
5. Personal/direct (addresses "you" directly)
6. Question (prompts self-reflection)
7. Number-led (e.g., "5 ways..." or "₹10,000 saved...")
8. Contrarian (challenges conventional wisdom)

For each: Subject line (under 50 chars) | Preview text (under 90 chars) |
Why this might outperform the original

Then recommend the TOP 2 to test first and explain your reasoning.
```

## 16.7 Content Repurposing Engine

```
I have a long-form content piece (blog post / webinar / report):
[PASTE CONTENT OR SUMMARY]

Topic: [TOPIC]
Brand voice: [PASTE VOICE SUMMARY]
Primary audience: [DESCRIBE]

Repurpose this into 6 derivative pieces:

1. LINKEDIN POST (250 words): Extract the most counterintuitive insight
2. TWITTER THREAD (8 tweets): The 7 most actionable points + CTA
3. EMAIL NEWSLETTER (400 words): Friendly breakdown of the key lesson
4. INSTAGRAM CAROUSEL (10 slides): Key statistics and insights as slides
   (For each slide: Slide title + 2–3 words of body text + visual suggestion)
5. YOUTUBE/PODCAST DESCRIPTION (150 words): SEO-optimized episode description
6. SHORT VIDEO SCRIPT (90 seconds): Hook + 3 insights + CTA (spoken word)

Ensure each piece:
- Can stand alone (reader doesn't need to have read the original)
- Matches the platform's native format and tone
- Drives back to the original piece with a natural CTA
- Maintains our brand voice throughout
```

## Hands-On Activities — Session 16

### Activity 16.1 — Brand Voice Establishment

**Objective:** Create a reusable brand voice summary for a real or hypothetical brand.

**Step 1:** Choose a brand — your company, a brand you work with, or a hypothetical brand you invent.

**Step 2:** Collect 3 samples of their existing content (website copy, social posts, emails).

**Step 3:** Run the Brand Voice Discovery Prompt.

**Step 4:** Write a 50-word brand voice summary and 3 specific "we always / we never" rules.

**Test:** Generate an Instagram caption and a LinkedIn post using your brand voice summary. Do they feel consistently on-brand?

---

### Activity 16.2 — Multi-Platform Content Sprint

**Objective:** Build a week of content for one brand using AI.

**Choose a topic** relevant to your brand (product launch, industry insight, how-to, etc.).

Generate:
- 1 LinkedIn thought leadership post
- 3 Instagram captions (different tones)
- 1 Twitter thread (7 tweets)
- 1 email newsletter section

**Evaluate consistency:** Read all pieces together. Does every piece sound like the same brand? Where does the voice drift?

---

### Activity 16.3 — Full Blog Post Production

**Objective:** Run the complete 4-step SEO blog workflow.

**Choose a topic** you genuinely know well (professional or personal expertise).

**Step 1:** Generate a keyword brief
**Step 2:** Write the introduction
**Step 3:** Write 2 body sections
**Step 4:** Write conclusion + meta copy

**Evaluate:** Is the final post something you would publish with minor edits? What would still need human refinement?

---

### Activity 16.4 — A/B Variant Generation

**Objective:** Build testing discipline into your content creation.

Take any real marketing asset you own or can access:
- An email subject line you've used
- A social media post
- A CTA button you've seen

Generate 8 A/B variants using the A/B Testing prompt.

**Analysis questions:**
- Which variant would you be most excited to test?
- Which does the AI think would win? Do you agree?
- What would you need to track to measure winner vs. loser?
