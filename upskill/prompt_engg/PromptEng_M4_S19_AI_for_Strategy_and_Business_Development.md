## 19.1 AI's Role in Strategy Work

Strategy work involves two types of thinking that AI can support in different ways:

```
TYPE 1 — ANALYTICAL (AI is very helpful):
• Structuring information into frameworks
• Generating options and alternatives
• Synthesizing research and competitive data
• Writing strategic documents and narratives
• Stress-testing plans through devil's advocate analysis

TYPE 2 — JUDGMENT (AI is a thinking partner, not the decision-maker):
• Which option is actually right for your company
• How much risk your organization can absorb
• Which stakeholders will resist and why
• Whether the culture can support the strategy
• Timing and sequencing decisions
```

**The strategic AI principle:** Use AI to do the thinking infrastructure — so that human judgment can focus on the judgment-intensive decisions that actually require it.

## 19.2 Strategic Analysis Frameworks

### SWOT Analysis

```
You are a strategy consultant who facilitates SWOT analyses for leadership teams.
You are known for producing SWOTs that are genuinely specific and actionable —
not generic statements that could apply to any company.

Conduct a SWOT analysis for [COMPANY/PRODUCT/INITIATIVE].

Context:
Company: [DESCRIBE]
Industry: [INDUSTRY]
Stage/size: [STAGE — startup / growth / mature enterprise]
Primary market: [GEOGRAPHY AND SEGMENT]
Current situation: [DESCRIBE WHAT'S HAPPENING — why is this SWOT being done?]
Key competitor: [WHO ARE THEY COMPETING AGAINST?]

SWOT format:

STRENGTHS (What we do better than competitors — specific and factual):
• [3–5 strengths — each with a "because..." explaining why it's a genuine strength]

WEAKNESSES (Where we are genuinely at a disadvantage — honest and specific):
• [3–5 weaknesses — each with a "which means..." showing the real consequence]

OPPORTUNITIES (External conditions we can capitalize on — with time horizon):
• [3–5 opportunities — each with: what it is | why now | how we could capture it]

THREATS (External forces that could harm us — with likelihood rating):
• [3–5 threats — each with: what it is | likelihood H/M/L | our vulnerability H/M/L]

STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS (the SWOT's value — what should we DO):
SO Strategy: Use Strengths to capture Opportunities (2 specific strategies)
ST Strategy: Use Strengths to mitigate Threats (2 specific strategies)
WO Strategy: Address Weaknesses to pursue Opportunities (2 specific strategies)
WT Strategy: Minimize Weakness exposure to Threats (2 specific strategies)

Specificity rule: If a SWOT point could be copy-pasted into any company's
analysis without change, it is too generic — replace with something specific.
```

### Porter's Five Forces

```
Apply Porter's Five Forces framework to analyze the competitive environment
of [INDUSTRY/MARKET SEGMENT].

For each force, provide:
- FORCE ASSESSMENT: High / Medium / Low pressure on profitability
- KEY FACTORS: 3–4 specific factors driving this force in this industry
- TREND: Is this force getting stronger, weaker, or stable? Why?
- IMPLICATION: What does this mean for strategy?

The Five Forces:

1. THREAT OF NEW ENTRANTS
2. BARGAINING POWER OF SUPPLIERS
3. BARGAINING POWER OF BUYERS/CUSTOMERS
4. THREAT OF SUBSTITUTE PRODUCTS/SERVICES
5. COMPETITIVE RIVALRY AMONG EXISTING PLAYERS

After analyzing all five forces:

OVERALL INDUSTRY ATTRACTIVENESS:
- Summary assessment (High / Medium / Low attractiveness for profitability)
- The most dominant force that shapes competition
- The force that is changing most rapidly and why

STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS:
- 3 specific strategies to reduce competitive pressure in this industry
- 1 "blue ocean" opportunity — a way to compete where forces are weaker

Industry context for this analysis: [DESCRIBE YOUR INDUSTRY AND SITUATION]
```

### PESTLE Analysis

```
Conduct a PESTLE analysis for [COMPANY] considering expansion into [MARKET/GEOGRAPHY].

For each dimension, provide:
- 3 specific factors (not generic statements — country/sector/time-specific)
- Assessment of each factor: Opportunity / Threat / Neutral
- Impact severity: High / Medium / Low
- Time horizon: Immediate (0–1yr) / Near (1–3yr) / Long (3+yr)

Dimensions:
P — POLITICAL (government stability, trade policy, regulation, political risk)
E — ECONOMIC (GDP, inflation, interest rates, purchasing power, currency risk)
S — SOCIAL (demographics, cultural factors, consumer behavior, workforce)
T — TECHNOLOGICAL (infrastructure, adoption rates, digital maturity, IP environment)
L — LEGAL (employment law, data protection, industry regulations, IP protection)
E — ENVIRONMENTAL (climate risk, sustainability regulations, ESG expectations)

SUMMARY TABLE:
| Factor | Dimension | Opportunity/Threat | Impact | Timeline |
[Generate one row per factor identified]

TOP 3 WATCH ITEMS: The three factors that most need active monitoring
GO / NO-GO RECOMMENDATION: Based on this analysis, is this expansion
advisable? With what conditions or mitigations?
```

## 19.3 Competitive Intelligence

### Competitor Profile Brief

```
Build a comprehensive competitive intelligence brief for [COMPETITOR NAME].

Context: We are [OUR COMPANY], competing in [MARKET SEGMENT].
Purpose: [WHY WE NEED THIS — new market entry / pitch prep / product strategy]

Competitor brief structure:

COMPANY OVERVIEW:
- Founded, HQ, employee count (approximate)
- Funding/ownership status and approximate valuation
- Core product/service offering (1–2 sentences)
- Primary target market segment

MARKET POSITIONING:
- Their stated positioning (how they describe themselves)
- Implied positioning (what they actually compete on)
- Price positioning: Premium / Mid-market / Value / Freemium

STRENGTHS (as their best customer would describe them):
[3–5 genuine strengths]

WEAKNESSES (as their most frustrated customer would describe them):
[3–5 genuine weaknesses — sourced from reviews, public feedback]

RECENT STRATEGIC MOVES:
[Last 12 months — product launches, funding, partnerships, hiring signals, pricing changes]

HOW THEY SELL:
- Sales motion: Enterprise / Mid-market / PLG (product-led growth) / Channel
- Primary acquisition channels
- Pricing model and typical deal size

HOW WE BEAT THEM:
- Our 3 best arguments against this competitor
- The customer type most likely to choose us over them
- Their most vulnerable accounts (where are they weakest?)

HOW THEY BEAT US:
- Their 3 best arguments against us (be honest)
- When we should walk away from a deal vs. fight for it

Flag all specific claims with [VERIFY] — confirm against current public sources.
```

### Market Entry Analysis

```
You are a market strategy consultant advising on expansion decisions.

Analyze the opportunity for [COMPANY] to enter [NEW MARKET].

Company profile: [DESCRIBE — size, current market, resources, capabilities]
New market: [DESCRIBE — geography, segment, what specifically]
Strategic rationale: [WHY are we considering this? Growth / defensiveness / following customers?]

Market Entry Analysis:

1. MARKET OPPORTUNITY ASSESSMENT
   - Market size and growth rate (flag for verification)
   - Addressable segment for our offering
   - Time to meaningful revenue (realistic estimate with rationale)

2. READINESS ASSESSMENT
   - What we have that transfers (capabilities, brand, relationships)
   - What we must build or acquire (gaps)
   - Estimated investment required to reach breakeven
   - Timeline to achieve meaningful market presence

3. ENTRY STRATEGY OPTIONS
   Option A: [e.g., Direct / Organic growth]
   Option B: [e.g., Partnership / Distribution]
   Option C: [e.g., Acquisition]
   For each: how to enter | time to market | cost | risk | success probability

4. COMPETITIVE DYNAMICS
   - Who we'll face and their response to our entry
   - First-mover vs. fast-follower considerations

5. KEY RISKS
   - Top 3 risks of entering
   - Top 3 risks of NOT entering

6. RECOMMENDATION
   - Go / Go with conditions / Wait / Don't go
   - Rationale (3–4 sentences)
   - If go: recommended entry option + top 3 priorities for first 90 days
```

## 19.4 Business Case Development

### Business Case Structure Generator

```
You are a management consultant who builds business cases for capital
investment and strategic initiatives. You ensure every business case is
investment-ready — clear, credible, and decision-enabling.

Build a business case framework for: [INITIATIVE NAME]

Initiative description: [DESCRIBE WHAT YOU'RE PROPOSING]
Decision-maker: [WHO APPROVES THIS — CEO / Board / Investment Committee]
Investment required: [APPROXIMATE AMOUNT]
Timeline: [WHEN RESULTS EXPECTED]
Strategic context: [WHY NOW — what strategic imperative does this address?]

Business case sections:

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (200 words max)
   - The decision being requested
   - The problem/opportunity being addressed
   - The recommended solution
   - Financial summary (investment, payback, ROI)
   - The recommendation

2. PROBLEM/OPPORTUNITY STATEMENT
   - Current state: what is happening now and why it's a problem
   - Cost of inaction: what happens if we don't act
   - The opportunity: what becomes possible if we do act

3. PROPOSED SOLUTION
   - What we are proposing (specific and detailed)
   - How it works
   - Why this solution vs. alternatives

4. FINANCIAL ANALYSIS
   - Investment required (one-time + ongoing)
   - Expected benefits (quantified — flag assumptions clearly)
   - Payback period
   - NPV / ROI (provide formula; I will insert actual numbers)
   - Sensitivity: what if key assumptions are 20% worse?

5. ALTERNATIVES CONSIDERED
   - 2–3 alternatives we assessed
   - Why we rejected each in favor of the proposed solution

6. RISKS AND MITIGATIONS
   - Top 5 risks (likelihood × impact matrix)
   - Specific mitigation for each

7. IMPLEMENTATION PLAN
   - Phases with milestones
   - Resource requirements
   - Dependencies

8. RECOMMENDATION AND DECISION REQUIRED
   - Clear recommendation
   - Specific decision needed from the approver
   - Decision deadline and why it matters

Note: Flag all quantitative claims with [ASSUMPTION: state what you assumed].
I will verify and replace with actual figures.
```

## 19.5 Business Development & Partnership

### Partnership Proposal Framework

```
Write a partnership proposal for [OUR COMPANY] to present to [POTENTIAL PARTNER].

Our company: [DESCRIBE — what we do, who we serve, our scale]
Potential partner: [DESCRIBE — what they do, who they serve, why they're attractive]
Partnership type: [Technology / Distribution / Co-marketing / Joint venture / Referral]
The mutual value: [WHY this partnership makes sense for BOTH parties]
The ask: [What specifically are you proposing?]

Partnership proposal structure:

1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW (1 page)
   - Partnership vision (1 sentence — what we'll achieve together)
   - Why [THEIR COMPANY] + [OUR COMPANY] = greater value than either alone
   - The 3 headline benefits for THEM (not for us — lead with their gain)

2. MARKET OPPORTUNITY WE'RE ADDRESSING TOGETHER
   - The customer problem this partnership uniquely solves
   - The market size and timing rationale

3. WHAT EACH PARTY BRINGS
   | Our Contribution | Their Contribution |
   | Key asset 1 | Key asset 1 |
   | Key asset 2 | Key asset 2 |
   | Key asset 3 | Key asset 3 |

4. PARTNERSHIP MECHANICS
   - How it works operationally (step by step)
   - Revenue / cost sharing model (framework — specific terms to be negotiated)
   - Governance and decision rights

5. PROJECTED OUTCOMES (year 1, year 2, year 3)
   For both parties: revenue opportunity, customer reach, strategic benefit
   Flag all projections as [ESTIMATE — to be validated jointly]

6. NEXT STEPS
   - 3-step path to partnership agreement
   - Our proposed timeline
   - The one decision we need from them to move forward
```

### Negotiation Preparation Prompt

```
You are an experienced business negotiator preparing me for an upcoming
negotiation with [COUNTERPARTY].

Negotiation context:
What we're negotiating: [DESCRIBE THE DEAL]
Our goal: [WHAT'S THE IDEAL OUTCOME FOR US?]
Their likely goal: [WHAT DO THEY WANT?]
Our BATNA (Best Alternative to Negotiated Agreement): [IF THIS DEAL FAILS, WHAT DO WE DO?]
Their likely BATNA: [IF WE DON'T DEAL, WHAT DO THEY DO?]
Our leverage: [WHERE DO WE HAVE POWER?]
Their leverage: [WHERE DO THEY HAVE POWER?]
Key points of potential conflict: [DESCRIBE]

Prepare my negotiation strategy:
1. ZONE OF POSSIBLE AGREEMENT (ZOPA): What's the realistic range of outcomes?
2. OPENING POSITION: Where should I start and why?
3. CONCESSION STRATEGY: What can I give up and in what order?
4. NON-NEGOTIABLES: What must I protect at all costs?
5. TACTICS THEY MAY USE: 3 tactics to expect and how to respond
6. MY OPENING STATEMENT: First 60 seconds — how I set the tone
7. KEY QUESTIONS TO ASK: 5 questions that reveal their real priorities
8. DEAL-KILLER DETECTOR: Signs this deal should be walked away from
```

## 19.6 Strategic Planning — Facilitation Support

### Strategy Workshop Agenda Generator

```
Design a [DURATION] strategy workshop agenda for [ORGANIZATION] leadership team.

Context:
Team: [WHO IS IN THE ROOM — titles, number of people]
Purpose: [WHAT DECISIONS MUST THIS WORKSHOP PRODUCE?]
Current situation: [WHAT'S HAPPENING — why is this workshop needed NOW?]
Pre-work completed: [Any analysis, surveys, or reading done beforehand]
Facilitator: [Internal leader / External consultant]

Workshop design:
1. AGENDA with time blocks (each block: duration | topic | method | output)
2. OPENING EXERCISE (15 min): Energy-setting and alignment activity
3. FRAMEWORKS TO USE: Which strategic tools are appropriate for this agenda
4. DECISION POINTS: The 3 specific decisions the team must make by end of day
5. PARKING LOT PROCESS: How to handle topics that need separate sessions
6. CLOSING: How to capture decisions and assign accountability
7. FOLLOW-UP: What happens in the 48 hours after the workshop

Output-driven design: Every block should produce a specific deliverable
(a decision, a ranked list, a framework, a draft strategy — not just discussion).
```

## Hands-On Activities — Session 19

---

### Activity 19.1 — Full SWOT for a Real Business

**Objective:** Build a genuinely specific SWOT that creates strategic insight.

Choose: your company, a company you're familiar with, or a public company.

**Step 1:** Run the SWOT Analysis prompt with full context.

**Step 2:** Evaluate the specificity of each point. Apply this test:
*"Could this exact bullet appear in a competitor's SWOT without changing a word?"*
If yes, it's too generic — improve it.

**Step 3:** Generate the 4 SO/ST/WO/WT strategic implications.

**Reflection:** Did the SWOT produce any strategies you hadn't considered before?

---

### Activity 19.2 — Competitive Intelligence Brief

**Objective:** Build a complete competitor profile for a company you compete with or study.

**Step 1:** Choose a real company (public or well-known).

**Step 2:** Run the Competitor Profile Brief prompt.

**Step 3:** Verify 5 specific claims against public sources (website, news, LinkedIn, Crunchbase).

**Deliverable:** A ready-to-use competitor brief with verified and flagged claims clearly marked.

### Activity 19.3 — Business Case Framework

**Objective:** Apply AI to a real investment or initiative you're considering.

Think of any initiative at work or personally:
- A technology tool you want to purchase
- A training program you want to run
- A new process you want to implement
- A market or product line you want to enter

Run the Business Case Structure Generator. Fill in all sections conceptually (use [ESTIMATE] where you don't have real numbers).

**Evaluate:** Would this business case convince a skeptical decision-maker? What's the weakest section?

### Activity 19.4 — Negotiation Preparation

**Objective:** Use AI to prepare for a real upcoming negotiation.

Think of any negotiation you have coming up (salary, vendor contract, project scope, partnership terms, resource allocation).

Run the Negotiation Preparation Prompt.

**Reflection:** What did the BATNA analysis reveal? What was your reaction to the concession strategy AI suggested — do you agree with the order?
