Your opening sets the entire tone. Prepare it word-for-word.

### Opening Script Framework

```
"Before I show you what I built, let me tell you what it cost me.

[Describe the "before" problem in 2 vivid sentences — make it real.
Time, frustration, quality, or opportunity cost.]

[Pause — let them feel it.]

In the next 10 minutes, I'm going to show you how I eliminated most of
that problem using AI prompt engineering — and give you the specific
prompts you can use to do the same thing."
```

**Why this opening works:**
- Opens with a relatable problem, not a title slide
- Creates immediate audience relevance
- Makes a clear promise (10 minutes → specific, useful takeaways)
- Sets up the narrative arc from Session 1

## 30.4 Handling the Q&A Panel

### Q&A Conduct Rules

```
WHEN A QUESTION IS ASKED:
1. Listen to the complete question before responding
   (Do not start answering while they are still talking)
2. Pause 2–3 seconds before answering
   (Shows you're thinking, not just reflexively responding)
3. Acknowledge the question in 1 sentence before answering
   ("That's exactly where I saw the most variance in results...")
4. Answer specifically — use data, examples, specific prompts
5. Close with confidence: "Does that answer your question?" only if
   you're genuinely uncertain you answered it fully

WHEN YOU DON'T KNOW THE ANSWER:
"That's a great test case I haven't tried yet. My hypothesis would be
[X] because [reason] — but I'd want to validate that before claiming it.
What I can tell you with certainty is [what you do know]."

WHEN YOU MADE A MISTAKE:
"You're right — I should have [X]. What I did instead was [Y] because
[genuine reason]. In retrospect, [honest reflection]. Here's what I'd
do differently now: [specific correction]."

NEVER:
✗ Bluff an answer you're not sure of — assessors know the material
✗ Dismiss a question as out of scope — engage with every question
✗ Say "I think" when you mean "I know" — and vice versa
✗ Apologize excessively — answer, don't hedge
```

### The Most Likely Q&A Questions

Based on common assessment panel patterns, these questions appear most often:

**Technique Questions:**
1. "Why did you choose [technique] for [task] rather than [alternative]?"
   → *Prepare: For each technique used, have a 2-sentence rationale.*

2. "How would you handle a case where your prompt produces inconsistent output?"
   → *Prepare: Your iteration and quality gate process.*

3. "What would you add to your pipeline if you had another two sessions?"
   → *Prepare: Your honest priority for the next feature.*

**Evidence Questions:**
4. "Your baseline measurement — how confident are you that it's accurate?"
   → *Prepare: Methodology for how you measured it; acknowledge limitations.*

5. "What would you need to see to conclude this doesn't work?"
   → *Prepare: Your honest failure conditions — shows intellectual integrity.*

**Responsible AI Questions:**
6. "What bias did you find in your outputs and how did you address it?"
   → *Prepare: Specific bias audit findings and specific guardrail added.*

7. "Who is accountable for errors from your system and how would you handle one?"
   → *Prepare: Your human-in-the-loop process and escalation path.*

**Scalability Questions:**
8. "Could your team of [N] use this without you?"
   → *Prepare: Your governance document and onboarding process.*

## 30.9 The Prompt Engineering Principles — Your Career Reference

Carry these 10 principles as your professional code for AI use:

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  THE 10 PRINCIPLES OF PROFESSIONAL PROMPT ENGINEERING               ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                      ║
║  1. SPECIFICITY OVER SPEED                                          ║
║     A precise prompt takes 5 more minutes and saves 30 minutes      ║
║     of unusable output. Always.                                     ║
║                                                                      ║
║  2. CONTEXT IS NOT OPTIONAL                                         ║
║     The AI knows nothing about your situation until you tell it.    ║
║     Every prompt starts with who, what, why, and for whom.         ║
║                                                                      ║
║  3. STRUCTURE BEFORE CONTENT                                        ║
║     Design the output format before asking for the content.        ║
║     Garbage structure produces garbage output every time.           ║
║                                                                      ║
║  4. ITERATE, DON'T TOLERATE                                         ║
║     If the output is wrong, the prompt is wrong.                   ║
║     Version it, change one thing, test again.                      ║
║                                                                      ║
║  5. VERIFY EVERYTHING CONSEQUENTIAL                                 ║
║     AI predicts text. Accountants, lawyers, and doctors verify.    ║
║     You are accountable for every claim you submit.                ║
║                                                                      ║
║  6. PIPELINE OVER MONOLITH                                          ║
║     Complex tasks belong in chains, not single prompts.            ║
║     Each step earns the right to the next step.                    ║
║                                                                      ║
║  7. DOCUMENT OR LOSE IT                                             ║
║     A prompt that isn't documented doesn't exist professionally.   ║
║     Your library is your institutional memory.                      ║
║                                                                      ║
║  8. BIAS IS YOUR RESPONSIBILITY                                     ║
║     You prompted the bias in. You are responsible for auditing     ║
║     it out. Ignorance is not a defense at professional scale.      ║
║                                                                      ║
║  9. HUMAN JUDGMENT AT THE CRITICAL JUNCTURES                       ║
║     Automate the volume. Reserve human attention for decisions      ║
║     that actually require it. Know the difference.                 ║
║                                                                      ║
║  10. TEACH WHAT YOU LEARN                                           ║
║     The fastest way to deepen your skill is to help someone        ║
║     else develop theirs. Prompt engineering compounds with sharing. ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
